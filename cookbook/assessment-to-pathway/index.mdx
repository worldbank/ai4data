# Practical Guide from the Readiness Assessment to an Improvement Pathway

**A cookbook for management and planning teams in national statistical organizations, and the advisers who support them.** Version 0.1 (working draft), October 2026.

:::note[Working draft]
This guide is a working draft from the AI for Data – Data for AI program. It
collects practical approaches and open standards. It does not
represent official World Bank Group guidance or policy and is not legal
advice; an organization applies it under its own statistical law, data
protection rules, and institutional policies.
Comments and corrections are welcome as
[GitHub issues](https://github.com/worldbank/ai4data/issues), and the
[contributing](./contributing.md) page explains how to propose a recipe.
:::

The AI-readiness assessment framework places a statistical organization on
twelve dimensions and says that its output is a prioritized map of where
the organization stands, where it needs to go, and what steps are most
likely to move it forward. This guide is the method for the second and
third parts of that sentence. It takes the sixty-nine answers of an
assessment and produces, in seven steps, a pathway that a board can
approve and a funder can read: the profile with the questions behind each
level, a target per dimension with a reason, the order of the work by
dependency, the effort and the kinds of cost, the pathway document, the
evidence that will show each question moved, and the comparison at the
re-assessment.

The guide follows the framework's own four-step logic of prioritize,
diagnose, plan, and monitor, and its pathway for early-stage
organizations, which puts the four foundations (quality control, metadata
standards, a searchable catalog, API access under a clear licence) before
the frontier dimensions. The other cookbooks of the series are where the
work of each dimension is done; this guide is where the work is chosen,
ordered, estimated, and measured. The [operational companion](/ai-readiness-in-practice)
maps each dimension to the cookbooks and tools that serve it, and the
[governance cookbook](/cookbook/governance-and-risk/) keeps the decision
log where the pathway's approvals and changes are recorded.

:::tip[What the scripts do and do not decide]
The scripts of this guide compute profiles, gaps, waves, estimates, and
comparisons from tables the organization fills. The targets, the
dependencies that differ from the running example's, the dates, and the
narrative are the organization's decisions, written in the same tables and
in the pathway document. A pathway is a set of recorded decisions with
arithmetic behind them.
:::

## Prerequisites

The recipes assume the following are in place or can be arranged:

- A completed AI-readiness assessment, with the answer and the assessor's note for each question, and the framework version it was made with.
- A planning team of two or three people who can run short Python scripts and who have the heads of units available for half a day each.
- For the AI-ready level, a governance board and a decision log (the [governance cookbook](/cookbook/governance-and-risk/decisions)) where the pathway's approval and its quarterly reviews are recorded.

## First steps

For a team with limited time, this sequence gives the most useful result first:

1. Compute the profile and read each dimension with its questions, following [chapter 1](./profile.mdx). This takes half a day.
2. Set a target per dimension with a reason and run the gap table, following [chapter 2](./targets.mdx). This takes half a day.
3. Compute the waves and the estimate, following [chapters 3](./sequence.mdx) and [4](./effort.mdx). This takes an hour.
4. Generate the pathway document, add the narrative and the dates, and take it to the board, following [chapter 5](./pathway.mdx). This takes a day.

The evidence register of [chapter 6](./evidence.mdx) is written in the first month of the pathway and checked each quarter, and the re-assessment of [chapter 7](./reassess.mdx) closes the cycle.

## Self-assessment

The [self-assessment](./start-here.mdx) takes about five minutes and places
an organization's planning practice at a level in each chapter.

## Maturity levels

Each chapter describes three levels. An organization can sit at different
levels in different chapters, and the first level is a complete, useful
state on its own.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | The tables exist and the scripts have run once; the pathway is approved and dated. |
| **AI-ready** | The tables are kept under version control, the register is checked each quarter, and changes to the pathway are recorded decisions. |
| **AI-native** | The profile comes from the assessment platform's export, the evidence pack is generated from the register, and the comparison is shared with the peer network. |

## Running example

The example organization was assessed on 2025-06-20 and scored 25.6 of
100, with ten dimensions at B and two at A. It set targets of C for the
foundations, licensing, strategy, and the legal environment, and B for
skills and agentic access; nine dimensions had a gap, forty-six questions
were below target, and the work fell into three waves for 75 to 149
person-days. Its evidence register was checked on 2026-01-15 with nothing
overdue, and its re-assessment on 2026-09-30 reached every target, with
an overall score of 55.3. All answers, targets, dates, and estimates are
invented; the dimensions and questions are those of the framework.

## Scope of the examples

The examples are small on purpose: one organization, one cycle, one
recipe per dimension in the estimate, so that a reader can run them in an
afternoon and see the shape of each step. They are starting points. Each
recipe ends with a **What this does not do** note that says where the
example stops and what the full version adds. The framework's own scoring
scripts are the reference for the published index; the profile script
here reproduces the published rule for planning.
