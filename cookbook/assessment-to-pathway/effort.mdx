# 4. Effort, roles, and kinds of cost

**Question:** What will the pathway take in effort, roles, and cost?

## Rationale

A pathway is approved and funded when the people who approve it know
what it will take. The cookbook series gives, for each recipe, a time
estimate and the role that carries it out, and the operational companion
sums them per dimension. This chapter does the same for one organization's
pathway: it selects the recipes that move each dimension from its current
level to its target, sums their person-days and roles, and names the kinds
of cost beyond staff time.

Three things are kept honest. The estimates are written for a first
implementation on the running examples of the cookbooks, and a large
catalog multiplies the per-item work. Money is given as kinds of cost,
because prices differ too much between countries and years for a figure to
hold. And the items the series does not cover, such as a legal review or a
recruitment, are listed as such, with their own estimate, so that the
pathway's total is the whole pathway.

## Target state

- Every dimension with a gap has the recipes that move it, with person-days, roles, and kinds of cost.
- The total effort, the roles, and the kinds of cost of the pathway are known.
- The items outside the cookbook series are listed with their own estimates.
- The estimate is in the pathway document and in the board's approval.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Fill the recipe effort table from the cookbooks and the organization's own estimates; run the estimate. |
| **AI-ready** | Approve the estimate with the targets and the waves; record actual days against it as the work proceeds. |
| **AI-native** | Re-estimate from the actual days of the previous cycle, and share the estimates with the peer network. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization's pathway selects nine recipes, one per
dimension with a gap, for 75 to 149 person-days. One item lies outside
the series: the legal review of the statistics act. The kinds of cost
beyond staff time are a gateway, hosting, legal fees, a share of the
model server, a search host, and a training venue. The file is
[recipe_effort.csv](pathname:///cookbook-files/assessment-to-pathway/recipe_effort.csv).
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="4.1 Fill the recipe effort table from the cookbooks" level="Foundational" skills="The planning team with the owners of the cookbook dimensions" time="Half a day">

**Result:** a table with one row per recipe that can move a dimension,
giving the level it reaches, a range of person-days, the roles, and the
kind of cost.

The table lists candidate recipes, more than the pathway will use, so
that the estimate can select among them when targets change. Each row
names the level the recipe reaches, because a recipe that brings a
dimension to B is already done for an organization at B, and a recipe
that reaches D is beyond a target of C.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `dimension`, `reaches` | The dimension the recipe serves and the level it brings it to |
| `recipe`, `cookbook` | What the recipe does and which cookbook it is in, or "outside the series" |
| `days_low`, `days_high` | A range of person-days for a first implementation |
| `roles` | The roles that carry it out, separated by semicolons |
| `cost_kind` | The kinds of cost, separated by semicolons; "Staff time" for every row |

```csv file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/assessment-to-pathway/recipe_effort.csv
```

1. For each dimension with a gap, take the recipes of the cookbook that
   serves it, with the time line of each recipe. The operational
   companion lists them per dimension.
2. Assign each recipe the level it reaches, from its own level in the
   cookbook: Foundational recipes reach B, AI-ready recipes C, AI-native
   recipes D.
3. Add the items the series does not cover, with the organization's own
   estimate and "outside the series" as the source.
4. Write the roles as the organization names them, so that the roles
   list of the estimate reads as a staffing list.

**Worked example.** The example organization's table has twenty-three rows
across the twelve dimensions. For metadata standards it lists the
completeness check (reaches B, one to two days), the code lists and
migration (reaches C, ten to twenty days), and regeneration from
production (reaches D, ten to fifteen days). For the legal environment it
lists the licence classification from the governance cookbook (reaches B)
and the legal review of the statistics act as an item outside the series
(reaches C, ten to twenty days, legal fees). For skills it lists the first
training cycle (reaches B) and a recruitment outside the series (reaches
C, salaries).

**What this does not do.** The table carries the cookbooks' estimates for
their running examples. The organization multiplies the per-item recipes
by its own counts of series, datasets, and records before it reads the
total as its own.

<Recipe title="4.2 Estimate the effort of the pathway" level="Foundational" skills="A planning officer who can run a Python script" time="Fifteen minutes">

**Result:** per dimension and in total, the recipes selected, their
person-days, the roles, and the kinds of cost beyond staff time, with
the items outside the series counted separately.

The script selects, for each dimension with a gap, the recipes whose
level is above the current level and at or below the target. It sums the
day ranges, unions the roles, and lists the kinds of cost. It reports a
dimension with a gap that no recipe serves, which is a gap in the table and a row to add.

1. Run the estimate with the score file, the targets, and the recipe
   effort table.
2. Read the total against the organization's staff. A total of 150
   person-days over a cycle of eighteen months is a part of one person's
   year for each of several roles, which the roles list names.
3. Read the kinds of cost against the budget. Each kind becomes a line
   in the pathway document with an owner.

```bash
python effort_estimate.py question_scores.csv targets.csv recipe_effort.csv
```

**Worked example.** The estimate selects one recipe per dimension, because
every gap is one level and each recipe reaches exactly the target. The
API takes the most, fifteen to thirty days, and licensing the least,
three to six. The total is 75 to 149 person-days, of which the legal
review is 10 to 20 outside the series. The roles list names twelve roles;
the developer and the dissemination lead appear in three dimensions each,
which tells the planning team where the cycle will queue. The kinds of
cost beyond staff time are six, each a budget line.

**What this does not do.** The estimate sums recipes. The calendar time
of the pathway depends on how many of the roles exist and how much of
their time the cycle gets, and the pathway document of chapter 5 is where
the dates are set.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Estimates from the investment view.** The operational companion's investment view sums effort and roles per dimension and level from the cookbooks; its figures can seed the recipe effort table.
- **Staff time as money.** Where the organization costs staff time, the person-days multiply by a daily rate it knows; the script leaves that multiplication to the organization.
- **Hardware and hosting.** The hosting notes of the operational companion give the systems by office size; the cost kinds here name them without prices.

## Common mistakes

- **Estimates read as the organization's own.** They are written for the running examples; the organization's counts multiply them.
- **Items outside the series left out.** The legal review and the recruitment are often the longest items of a pathway.
- **Prices in the pathway document.** A figure from another country and year is wrong by the time it is read; kinds of cost with owners hold.
- **Roles summed without names.** Twelve roles in a list is a staffing question the board has to see.

## Verification

- **Estimate check.** The script passes: every dimension with a gap has at least one recipe.
- **Coverage check.** For a dimension picked at random, the selected recipes read in order bring it from its current level to its target.
- **Budget check.** Every kind of cost in the output has a line and an owner in the pathway document.
