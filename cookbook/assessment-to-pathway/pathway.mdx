# 5. The improvement pathway document

**Question:** How is the pathway written so that it can be agreed and funded?

## Rationale

The framework's third planning step is to plan: produce the tailored
improvement pathway, sequencing actions by priority and feasibility. The
pathway is a document because that is what a board approves, a ministry
funds, and a development partner reads. It has a fixed shape so that each
of those readers finds what they look for: a summary with the score, the
gaps, the waves, and the effort; the profile; the targets with their
reasons; the sequence; the effort and roles by wave; the evidence and
re-assessment; and the governance of the pathway itself.

The document is generated from the tables of chapters 1 to 4, for the
same reason the statement of model use is generated from the register:
a document written by hand drifts from the tables it summarizes, and a
generated one cannot. The planning team adds what the tables do not hold,
the narrative of the organization's choices and the dates a budget cycle
imposes, in the document the script produces.

## Target state

- The pathway document exists with its seven sections, generated from the current tables.
- The planning team's narrative and the dates are added, and the document is approved by the board and dated.
- The document is the reference for the evidence register of chapter 6 and the re-assessment of chapter 7.
- Changes to targets, waves, or estimates are made in the tables and the document is regenerated.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Generate the document, add the narrative and the dates, and approve it. |
| **AI-ready** | Regenerate the document at each quarter's board meeting with the evidence register's status, and keep every version. |
| **AI-native** | Publish the pathway, or its summary, with the statement of model use, and share it with the peer network. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization's pathway document was generated from its
tables and approved by its board in September 2025. Its summary reads:
25.6 of 100 overall, nine dimensions with a gap in three waves, 75 to 149
person-days across nine recipes, one item outside the series.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="5.1 Generate the pathway document from the tables" level="Foundational" skills="A planning officer who can run a Python script" time="Fifteen minutes">

**Result:** a Markdown document with the seven sections, every table of
which equals the output of the scripts of chapters 1 to 4.

The generator reads the score file, the targets, the dependencies, and
the recipe effort table, and writes the sections in order. The summary
paragraph is written from the numbers so that the first sentence a reader
sees is the one the tables support.

1. Run the generator with the organization's name and the assessment
   date.
2. Read the document once as a reader of each kind would: the board for
   the targets and the effort, the funder for the kinds of cost and the
   waves, the units for their own dimension's recipes.
3. Where something is wrong, correct the table and regenerate. The
   document is never edited by hand before the narrative step of recipe
   5.2.

```bash
python pathway_document.py question_scores.csv targets.csv dependencies.csv recipe_effort.csv --organization "National Statistics Office (example)" --assessed 2025-06-20
```

```text
# Improvement pathway: National Statistics Office (example)

Assessed on 2025-06-20 with the AI-readiness assessment framework.

## Summary

The organization scores 25.6 of 100 overall (pillar I 0.73, pillar II 0.80 on the 0 to 3 scale). 9 of 12 dimensions have a gap to their target, addressed in 3 waves. The pathway needs 75 to 149 person-days across 9 recipes, of which 1 item lie outside the cookbook series.
...
```

**Worked example.** The example organization's document opens with the summary above. It continues with the profile table of chapter 1 and the targets and gaps of chapter 2 with their reasons. Then come the three waves of chapter 3 with the "after" dependencies, and the effort by wave of chapter 4 as a table with one row per selected recipe. The evidence
section says that every question below target has a row in the evidence
register, and the governance section names the head of methodology as
the owner of the pathway and the board as the body that changes it.

**What this does not do.** The generator writes what the tables hold. The
narrative, the dates, and the signatures are added in recipe 5.2, and a
regenerated document has to have them added again, which is why the
narrative is kept as its own short file.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.2 Add the narrative and the dates and take the pathway to the board" level="Foundational" skills="The planning team writes; the board approves" time="One day to write; one meeting to approve">

**Result:** the pathway document carries the organization's explanation of
its choices, the dates of each wave, the owners of each kind of cost, and
the board's approval.

The generated tables say what will be done. The narrative says why the
organization chose it: which users or obligations press on which
dimensions, what the previous cycle learned, what was left out and when
it will be taken up. The dates come from the budget cycle and the staff
available: a wave gets a start and an end, and each kind of cost gets an
owner and the budget line it will sit on.

1. Write a narrative of two or three paragraphs per section where the
   tables need explanation, kept in a separate file so that it survives
   regeneration.
2. Give each wave a start and an end, from the estimate and the roles'
   availability. A wave whose roles do not exist yet starts after the
   recruitment or the training that creates them.
3. Give each kind of cost an owner and a budget line.
4. Submit the document to the board with the gap table, the sequence,
   and the estimate as annexes, and record the approval in the decision
   log of the governance cookbook.

**Worked example.** The example organization's narrative explains two choices. Licensing went into the first wave because the legal adviser had capacity in the autumn and the licence matrix is the evidence for a whole dimension. The technical environment stayed at B because the model server the organization already runs is enough for the components of the cycle. The first wave
runs from October 2025 to March 2026, the second from January to
September 2026 with the API as the long item, and the third from July to
September 2026. The gateway, the hosting, and the search host sit on the
IT budget line owned by the head of IT; the legal fees on the legal
adviser's line; the training venue on the methodology unit's line. The
board approved the pathway in September 2025 and recorded the decision.

**What this does not do.** Approval starts the pathway. Whether it is
followed is what the evidence register of chapter 6 shows each quarter,
and the dates in the document move by decisions of the board recorded in
the log.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Document format.** Markdown is what the generator writes and what version control keeps readable. The organization's template for board papers can take the sections in the same order.
- **Annexes.** The gap table, the sequence, and the estimate are annexes; the evidence register of chapter 6 joins them at each quarter's version.
- **Funding proposals.** A funder's template usually asks for objectives, activities, inputs, and indicators; the targets are the objectives, the recipes the activities, the kinds of cost the inputs, and the evidence register the indicators.

## Common mistakes

- **A document written from scratch.** It drifts from the tables within a quarter.
- **A narrative inside the generated file.** It is lost at the next regeneration; a separate file survives.
- **Dates set from the estimate alone.** The roles' availability sets the calendar.
- **Approval without a record.** A pathway whose approval nobody can find has the standing of an intention.

## Verification

- **Generation check.** The generator runs on the current tables and the document's tables equal the scripts' outputs.
- **Completeness check.** Every wave has dates, every kind of cost an owner, and the narrative file is present.
- **Approval check.** The approval is in the decision log with the document's version and date.
