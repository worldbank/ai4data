# 1. Scope of model assistance

**Question:** Which curation tasks can a model assist, and which stay with people?

## Rationale

Curation tasks differ in what a wrong output costs. A model that drafts
a keyword list costs a curator a minute to correct. A model that rewrites
a definition can change the meaning of a series for every future user,
and nobody may notice for years. The first decision is therefore which
tasks a model may take, in which role, and which decisions stay with a
person. The roles are few: a model can draft text, flag a possible
problem, align text with a vocabulary, translate, or summarize, and in
every case someone decides what happens next.

The second decision is what may be sent to a model at all. A model run
by a provider receives whatever text is sent to it. A record of a public
series is public, and sending it is no concern. An unpublished record or
a confidential microdata dictionary is a different matter, and the rule
has to be written before anyone is tempted. This chapter writes both
decisions down and chooses the records on which to try the workflow.

## Target state

- A typology lists the curation tasks, the role a model takes in each, and the decision that remains with a curator.
- A rule states that nothing reaches the published catalog without a named curator's approval, and that confidential or unpublished records are never sent to external services.
- The records to start with are chosen from use and from completeness, and the team knows what success looks like for them.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write the typology and the two rules. Choose twenty records to start with. |
| **AI-ready** | Add decision rights per field (which fields a model may draft, which it may only flag). Record the model, provider, and data sensitivity in the AI component register. |
| **AI-native** | Review the scope each period from acceptance rates and incidents, and extend or restrict the tasks accordingly. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization allows a model to draft definitions and keywords,
to flag inconsistencies in any field, and to propose vocabulary mappings,
for its five public indicator records. Units and methodology text may be
flagged but never drafted. Every suggestion goes to one curator, who
records a decision.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="1.1 Write the task typology with decision rights" level="Foundational" skills="Head of the curation unit" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the curation unit has a one-page table that says, for each
kind of curation task, what a language model is allowed to do and what
a curator decides.

Curation work falls into a few families of task: filling a missing
field, proposing keywords, flagging a field that disagrees with another,
translating, summarizing. For each family, the organization decides the
model's role. A model that *drafts* writes text for a curator to accept
or change. A model that *flags* points at a possible problem and
proposes a fix. In both cases the curator decides. The table below is a
starting typology:

| Task | Model role | Curator decides |
|---|---|---|
| Missing description or definition | Draft from the record and the schema | Accept, edit, or reject the draft |
| Keywords and topics | Draft; map free text to the vocabulary | Confirm the mapping |
| Name against definition, unit against values | Flag with a severity and a proposed fix | Whether the flag is right and what the fix is |
| Methodology and limitations text | Flag only | Any change |
| Translations of labels | Draft, marked machine-translated | Native-speaker review before publication |
| Summaries for catalog pages | Draft | Accept or edit |

1. Start from the table and adapt it to the organization's fields and
   risks. The program's
   [metadata-quality work](/docs/metadata-quality/generative-ai-for-metadata-quality)
   names the same task families: field generation, semantic alignment,
   vocabulary standardization, translation, summarization, and
   diagnostics.
2. Mark which fields a model may never draft (units, methods, legal
   text) and which it may only flag.
3. Name the curator role that decides, and where decisions are recorded
   ([chapter 5](./review.mdx), recipe 5.3).

**Worked example.** The example organization's typology follows the
table above with two changes. Its curators decided that a model may
draft keywords and definitions for the five indicator records, may flag
any field, and may propose vocabulary mappings. The unit and the
methodology text are listed as "flag only", because the unit of the
under-five mortality rate was once changed by a well-meaning editor and
took a year to notice. Translations are allowed as drafts into French,
with the native-speaking statistician named as the reviewer. The senior
metadata curator is named as the person who decides on every
suggestion, and the decisions file of chapter 5 is named as the record.

**What this does not do.** The typology sets roles. It does not make
drafts good; [chapters 3](./draft.mdx) and [4](./assess.mdx) measure
them.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.2 Set the confidentiality and approval rules" level="Foundational" skills="Head of the curation unit; legal" time="One day">

**Result:** two written rules exist that every staff member and every
pipeline follows. The first says which records may be sent to which
model. The second says what has to happen before a model's output reaches
the published catalog.

A model run by a provider receives whatever text is sent to it. For a
public catalog record that is no concern; for an unpublished record or
a microdata dictionary it may be. A model the organization runs on its
own server receives nothing that leaves the building.

1. Confidentiality: only public records, or records cleared for
   publication, are sent to a provider's model. Unpublished records and
   microdata dictionaries go only to models the organization runs
   itself. Record the rule in the AI-use policy of the
   [dissemination cookbook](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/govern).
2. Approval: a model's output reaches the published catalog only after a
   named curator records a decision. Until then it is marked as model
   output (a `draft` status, or a note field).
3. Register the pipeline (model, provider, data used, owner, review
   date) in the AI component register. The program's tools accept any
   provider, including a local model, so the rule can be met either way.

**Worked example.** The example organization's confidentiality rule
reads: "Records of published series and published surveys may be sent to
the hosted model named in the register. Records that are not yet
published, and every microdata dictionary, are processed only by the
open-weight model on the organization's own server." Its approval rule
reads: "A model's suggestion is stored with the status draft and the
model's name. It becomes part of the published record only when the
senior metadata curator records accept or edit in the decisions file."
The register entry for the pipeline names the hosted model and the
local model. It names the public catalog records as the data and the
head of the curation unit as the owner, and it sets a review date six
months out.

**What this does not do.** Rules state the practice. The review board
and the decision log of [chapter 5](./review.mdx) are where the practice
is visible and auditable.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.3 Choose the records to start with" level="Foundational" skills="Curator; dissemination officer" time="Two hours">

**Result:** the unit has a first set of about twenty records on which
model assistance is worth the review effort, and a written definition of
what success on them looks like.

1. Take the records with the highest use (the
   [monitoring cookbook](/cookbook/monitoring-data-use/) counts it) and
   the lowest completeness (the
   [completeness check](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/find) at the
   AI-ready level lists the missing fields).
2. Start with twenty: enough to measure acceptance, few enough to review
   every suggestion.
3. Define success in three numbers: the share of drafts accepted or
   edited, the share of flags the curator confirms, and the completeness
   gain after the decisions are applied.
4. Record the baseline (completeness, curator time per record) before
   the first run, so that the gain can be measured.

**Worked example.** The example organization has only five indicator
records in the running example, so its start set is those five plus
fifteen survey records chosen from the monitoring report's list of most
used datasets. The completeness check at the AI-ready level shows that
the five indicators lack between two and eight fields each and that the
unemployment rate has no definition at all. The baseline reads:
completeness 0 of 20 records at the AI-ready level, and 25 minutes of
curator time per record when a definition is written from scratch. The
definition of success reads: at least 70 percent of drafts accepted or
edited, at least 80 percent of flags confirmed, and at least 15 of the
20 records complete at the AI-ready level after the decisions are
applied.

**What this does not do.** Twenty records calibrate the workflow. The
catalog-scale run comes after the acceptance rates of
[chapter 8](./improve.mdx) are known.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Typology:** the task families of the program's metadata-quality work; roles draft, flag, align, translate, summarize.
- **Rules:** the AI-use policy template and the AI component register of the dissemination cookbook.
- **Models:** hosted or local through a provider-agnostic client (litellm, as the program's tools use); local models for anything unpublished.
- **Record selection:** use counts from monitoring and completeness from the profile check.

## Common mistakes

- **Scope by tool.** The question is which tasks, in which role; the tool follows.
- **Pasting unpublished records into a public assistant.** The first rule exists for this.
- **Drafting the method text.** A model that rewrites the methodology changes the series' meaning; flag only.
- **Starting with the whole catalog.** Review capacity decides the scale; twenty records first.

## Verification

- **Typology.** Every curation task in the team's procedures has a row with a role and a decision.
- **Rules.** Staff can state both rules, and the register lists the pipeline with an owner.
- **Start set.** Twenty records have been chosen with their use and completeness recorded, and a baseline exists.
