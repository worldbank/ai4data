# 7. Standards-Informed Schema Refinement

**Question:** How can standards improve an artifact-derived schema while preserving its established requirements?

:::note[Structural draft]
This chapter establishes learning milestones and candidate recipes. Detailed
procedures, worked examples, executable exercises, and implementation checks
remain for later authoring. Intended results below describe planned recipes.
:::

## Rationale

A standards crosswalk compares local fields with established terms and patterns. Exact, close, broader, narrower, structural, and unmatched relationships have different implications. A mapping alone does not establish application fit.

Standards review follows artifact-driven development and validation. Humans accept changes that improve semantic accuracy, reject mappings that distort observed concepts, and defer structures beyond the chosen representation.

## Target state

- Mappings identify authoritative terms and their relationship to local meanings.
- Accepted, rejected, and deferred proposals have explicit reasons.
- The final specification preserves descriptive requirements and states its evaluation boundary.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret a standards relationship without automatic substitution.
- Explain derivation versus attribution and separate the final flat schema from post-study implementation.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 6 provides validation findings and adjudicated scope. The practitioner can compare definitions and distinguish conceptual coverage from representation.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Compare selected definitions with authoritative standards under human review. |
| **AI-ready** | Record crosswalk relationships, sources, decisions, and the versioned specification. |
| **AI-native** | Use interactive AI assistance for source research and mappings, with human verification and acceptance. |

A readable crosswalk and flat specification preserve the core reasoning. Post-study Pydantic and JSON Schema implementations can enforce richer constraints, but their structural and presentation changes require separate evaluation.

## Common mistakes

- A close relationship can be treated as exact equivalence.
- A vocabulary can narrow a local concept beyond its artifact evidence.
- The final specification can be described as independently validated after standards refinement.
