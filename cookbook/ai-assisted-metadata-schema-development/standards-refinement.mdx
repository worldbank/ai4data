# 7. Standards-Informed Schema Refinement

**Question:** How can standards improve an artifact-derived schema while preserving its established requirements?

## Rationale

Artifact-driven development establishes the information that a schema must
describe. Standards review adds evidence about established meanings, terminology,
and representation practices. It can expose an overloaded field or a useful
distinction even when held-out validation has not warranted an additional
descriptive requirement. Start with the operational specification and human
decisions retained after [chapter 6](./validation.mdx). In the case study, that
was documentation-revised v1.1.1 with 36 fields.

A standards crosswalk is a documented comparison between local fields and
external terms or patterns. Its relationships help reviewers reason about
possible changes. They do not replace artifact evidence or decide whether the
application should adopt a name, vocabulary, or structure. Humans preserve the
observed requirements while accepting useful refinements, rejecting unsuitable
restrictions, and distinguishing implemented changes from deferred structures.
Choose standards by the resources, uses, and representation problems they
address. DCMI, Schema.org, PROV-O, and the other reviewed sources demonstrate
that selection for Data Snapshots; none is a universal requirement for every
artifact class.

## Target state

- A crosswalk identifies authoritative terms, compared meanings, source versions or dates, and bounded relationships.
- A decision record explains accepted, partially accepted, rejected, and deferred recommendations.
- A versioned final specification reconciles field names and cardinalities while stating what remains untested.

## Learning objectives

- The practitioner can compare a local meaning with an external term without treating similar names as equivalent.
- The practitioner can justify a standards decision using artifact evidence, application needs, and representation limits.
- The practitioner can explain the provenance split and reconcile the 36-field baseline with the 37-field endpoint.
- The practitioner can distinguish semantic alignment, formal compliance, and downstream implementation.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 6 supplies the documentation-revised schema, human validation decisions,
and representation backlog. The reviewer understands the unit described and its
intended cataloging and discovery uses. Prepared records support manual exercises
without model calls. Rechecking external terms requires access to their
authoritative documentation.

## Recipes

<Recipe title="7.1 Build an evidence-backed standards crosswalk" level="AI-ready" skills="Metadata practitioner; standards reviewer" time="60–90 minutes for the selected field comparisons">

**Result:** A crosswalk records each compared local meaning, authoritative term, scope relationship, possible refinement, and uncertainty for human review.

Different standards describe different kinds of resources and relationships.
A field naming a snapshot and a field naming its parent can both resemble a
title property while referring to different objects. Similarly, a useful
provenance relation may require entities and agents that a flat text field
does not represent. Comparison therefore begins with definitions, resource
scope, and evidence requirements, before any proposed replacement.

**Worked example.** The following selected comparisons retain the recorded
relationship judgments. Their explanatory notes are teaching summaries checked
against the linked authoritative sources.

| v1.1.1 field | External term and original scope | Recorded comparison and implication |
|---|---|---|
| title; source_document_title | [DCMI title](https://www.dublincore.org/specifications/dublin-core/dcmi-terms/terms/title/) names the resource being described. | Exact, with different resource subjects: the snapshot for title and its parent for source_document_title. Preserve that boundary. |
| subject_domain | [DCMI subject](https://www.dublincore.org/specifications/dublin-core/dcmi-terms/terms/subject/) describes resource topics. | Standard broader: a thematic domain is a more specific use of subject description. A plural local field can preserve multiple domains without mandatory external coding. |
| variable_name | [Schema.org variableMeasured](https://schema.org/variableMeasured) describes measured dataset variables through text or structured descriptions. | Close: the snapshot field includes represented variables, indicators, and metrics while keeping a flat value representation. Preserve the local scope. |
| data_source | [PROV-O wasDerivedFrom](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/#wasDerivedFrom) relates an object to another object used to produce it; [wasAttributedTo](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/#wasAttributedTo) connects an object to its responsible actor. | Related or structural: the local field combined represented-data origins and credited production. The distinction motivates recipe 7.2. |
| project_name | [Schema.org Project](https://schema.org/Project) describes a planned enterprise and is an Organization type. | Standard narrower in the study's comparison: the local definition also covers programs, operations, and initiatives. Avoid imposing the external class's scope on every local value. |

The Project judgment is an application-specific comparison; it does not assert
a formal subclass relationship. Likewise, “no direct match” concerns the sources
reviewed in this study. It does not prove that no useful term exists elsewhere.

1. Freeze the complete starting specification and identify standards relevant to the artifact class, intended uses, and unresolved semantic or structural questions. The study's selections are listed on the [standards page](./standards.md); another class may need different references, and no single vocabulary must cover every field.
2. Locate authoritative term definitions and usage notes. Record the term identifier, documentation URL, version or publication date where available, and access date for mutable pages. Inspect the kind of resource and value expected by the term.
3. Compare those meanings with the local definition and artifact evidence. State whether the external term is broader, narrower, close, or otherwise related. Explain the direction and the scope difference; a shared name alone is insufficient.
4. Save a working copy of the new [standards-review worksheet](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/standards_review_template.csv). Record relationship and candidate_refinement separately from decision and decision_reason. Preserve uncertainty for review, and retain a reference to any eventual specification change.

The worksheet is a new teaching aid. Its local_meaning and scope_difference
columns record the comparison; authoritative_url, source_version_or_date, and
checked_date make the external evidence traceable. The remaining columns connect
that evidence to a human reviewer, disposition, and output. It is not a recovered
historical ledger or an automated mapping contract.

**Recorded crosswalk categories.** The study summarized the 36 baseline fields
as follows. These are descriptive review categories. They do not assert formal
mapping predicates or a machine-enforced relation vocabulary.

| Recorded relationship | Fields | Meaning for this review |
|---|---:|---|
| Exact | 10 | The reviewed semantics correspond at the identified resource level. |
| Close | 11 | The comparison is useful but a bounded local difference remains. |
| Standard broader | 6 | The external meaning covers more than the local field. |
| Standard narrower | 3 | The external meaning covers only part of the local requirement. |
| Related or structural | 1 | The external pattern suggests distinctions or relationships without a direct field equivalent. |
| No direct match | 5 | The reviewed sources supply no direct counterpart for the local meaning. |
| Total | 36 | Each baseline field appears once in this summary; a field can have several candidate external correspondences. |

**Verification.** Reconcile 10 + 11 + 6 + 3 + 1 + 5 = 36. For a selected row,
identify the local definition, authoritative term, described resource, scope
difference, and proposed consequence. The [standards page](./standards.md)
records the term-check date separately from historical study source dates.
Each suggestion must remain distinguishable from an accepted change.

**What this does not do.** A crosswalk does not establish formal conformance,
lossless conversion, or measured interoperability. The study's standards review
was interactive and multi-turn, with human adjudication. It was not a single
fixed-prompt inference, deterministic runner, or automatic acceptance process.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.2 Adjudicate candidate standards refinements" level="Foundational" skills="Domain reviewer; metadata designer" time="60–90 minutes for the provenance split and contrasting decisions">

**Result:** A decision record distinguishes accepted semantic refinements from rejected restrictions and deferred structures, with evidence and reasons for each disposition.

A useful external distinction still needs an operational decision. Reviewers
consider semantic accuracy, artifact evidence, cataloging and discovery needs,
annotation consistency, and maintenance cost. A proposal may improve terminology
while adding relationships the flat specification cannot preserve. Partial
acceptance allows that distinction to remain visible without pretending that
the richer structure has been implemented.

**Input example: the combined provenance field.** The baseline v1.1.1 field was
data_source. Its exact definition and accompanying guidance were:

```markdown
The named dataset, survey, publication, organization, or credited agent from which the represented data originate or which is explicitly credited with producing the snapshot artifact.

In v1.1.1, `data_source` may retain multiple names but does not structurally distinguish roles such as data source, data producer, map maker, preparer, or contributor. A role-bearing representation is deferred to v1.2. Do not copy the parent document's authors or publisher into this field solely because they are associated with the document; the source or attribution must be explicitly relevant to the snapshot or its represented data.
```

The wording deliberately combined data origin and explicitly credited
production in one field. Here, an agent is a responsible actor, such as a
credited person or organization. Standards review supplied a way to distinguish
the source and credit meanings.
In [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/#wasDerivedFrom), derivation relates
entities; [attribution](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/#wasAttributedTo) assigns
responsibility to an agent. [DCMI source](https://www.dublincore.org/specifications/dublin-core/dcmi-terms/terms/source/)
also concerns a resource used in deriving another resource. Those distinctions
support separate local fields without requiring a provenance graph.

**Trace the distinction to an artifact.** The [Afghanistan income table](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4data/data-snapshot/blob/626f3c54ba6d8f6e0ba0795f9e544e24b2650faf/snapshots/unhcr/108733-revised-public-wb-unhcr-policy-brief-final_table_000.png),
108733-revised-public-wb-unhcr-policy-brief-final_table_000.png, carries the
source phrase “Authors’ calculation based on ALCS 2013–14.” The
[source register](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/source_images.csv) retains its external image link and
visible credit, and provenance-split in [evidence.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/evidence.json)
preserves the discovery observations and later decision.

1. Compare the recommendation with the complete baseline definition and validation findings. State whether it changes a field's name, meaning, value guidance, or structure. Explain which observed requirement is preserved.
2. Evaluate benefit and cost against the artifact-derived requirements. Adopt constraints when they fit the intended meanings and supported values; reject restrictions or normalization that would distort them. Assess structural changes against the representation the application needs, and keep interoperability opportunities separate from measured results.
3. Record the human disposition and rationale. The research used Accept, Partially accept, Reject, and Defer, sometimes combining an accepted flat change with deferred objects. Preserve those labels when reporting its decisions.
4. Connect accepted wording to the output version. Keep rejected and deferred recommendations inspectable. For a new review, record the actual reviewer and any unresolved evidence requirement; do not invent a historical participant or decision date.

**Worked example: the provenance split.** The recorded decision was **Accept split; defer objects**. The final v1.1.2
definitions are reproduced exactly below.

| Final field | Cardinality | Exact definition |
|---|---|---|
| data_sources | 0..* | The named datasets, surveys, publications, or other entities from which the represented data originate. |
| attributions | 0..* | The agents explicitly credited with producing or contributing to the snapshot artifact. |

Cardinality states how many values a field may hold. The notation 0..* means
an optional ordered list of text values in this flat specification. Final guidance preserves explicit attribution roles together
with the credited agent as source-grounded text. It does not authorize copying
parent-document authors or publishers solely because they are associated with
the document. A role-bearing entity structure was deferred.

| Final field | Printed value transcribed from the paper's Appendix H.1 |
|---|---|
| data_sources | ALCS 2013–14 |
| attributions | Authors’ calculation |

The first value names the source data. The second preserves the explicit credit
and calculation role; it does not resolve “Authors” into named agents. These
are a two-field transcription of the paper's printed values, with no new model
run or independent extraction evaluation. A complete typed record remains
outside the example.

Separating the roles makes a reviewer's interpretation more precise: a credited
calculation is not itself the underlying survey. It does not establish measured
retrieval or extraction improvement. The split followed standards comparison;
it was not an additional missing field accepted during held-out validation.

**Contrasting recorded decisions.** The following are selected outcomes from
the research decision matrix. The explanations summarize its documented reasons.

| Candidate recommendation | Recorded disposition | Reason and flat-schema outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Split data_source into data_sources and attributions; introduce role-bearing objects. | Accept split; defer objects. | Preserve the useful semantic distinction in flat source-grounded text; richer provenance relationships remain later work. |
| Narrow project_name to Schema.org Project's scope. | Reject. | Keep projects, programs, operations, and initiatives within the local field. Optional external mappings can be used where applicable. |
| Rename panel_title to panel_titles and introduce panel objects. | Partially accept. | Retain explicit repeated headings in the plural flat field; defer panel objects and their relationships. |
| Rename subject_domain to subject_domains and require external coding. | Partially accept. | Represent multiple coequal domains while rejecting a mandatory vocabulary that cannot cover all artifacts. |

These decisions differ because the recommendations have different consequences.
A semantic distinction can improve a flat field boundary. A narrower external
class can discard a required local meaning. An object model can be valuable
while exceeding the study's directly inspectable representation. None is
settled by matching a name or counting external references.

**Verification.** Match the provenance decision and two final definitions to
provenance-split. In the worksheet, record the candidate, disposition, reason,
and output reference separately. Explain why “Authors’ calculation” is credited
production and ALCS is data origin, without inferring agent identities. Confirm
that rejecting the Project restriction preserves project_name's local scope.

**What this does not do.** The fields are informed by provenance semantics;
they do not implement PROV-O's entity-agent relations or demonstrate compliance.
Flat text does not identify agent resources or encode structured attribution
roles. The selected matrix rows are not a complete historical conversation log,
and the new worksheet is a teaching aid.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="7.3 Reconcile refinements and prepare a publication record" level="AI-ready" skills="Metadata designer; documentation reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for inventory reconciliation and a publication checklist">

**Result:** The supplied exercise produces an inventory reconciliation, a review of selected standards-informed changes, and publication-readiness and decision-record notes. Applying the complete procedure to another artifact collection produces a complete, versioned operational specification with field definitions, guidance, and an auditable refinement record.

Publication makes the schema usable and its reasoning reviewable. A list of
new names alone cannot show whether coverage was preserved, whether a field
split introduced a semantic distinction, or whether deferred structures were
implemented. The specification and decision record must therefore travel
together, with the starting version available for comparison.

The [final field inventory](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/final_field_inventory.csv) transcribes baseline and final field names and final cardinalities. It supports the exercise below alongside the selected definitions and decisions in recipes 7.1–7.2; it is not a full schema or machine-readable validator.

1. Identify the baseline and final versions in the supplied inventory and locate the selected decisions that explain their differences.
2. Reconcile the before-and-after field inventory and review the highlighted changes below. Distinguish renames, definition changes, and the provenance split. Keep the crosswalk separate from the human decision record.
3. Prepare publication-readiness and decision-record notes. Identify the evidence and disposition supporting each selected change, deferred requirements, and further validation needs. State which complete specifications and checks a publication would still require. Publication of this cookbook remains a separate review step.

**Application to another artifact collection.** Retain the complete starting
specification and approved standards decisions. Apply accepted changes to a new
version while preserving source-supported descriptive requirements. Review every
renamed or revised field's definition and annotation guidance, carrying forward
evidence boundaries, applicability, and missing-value rules. Reconcile the full
inventory and retain an auditable decision record with authoritative references,
reviewers, and unresolved requirements. The complete, versioned operational
specification and its supporting record should be reviewed together before
publication. A plural name alone does not establish relationships among values.

**Worked example: reconcile the field inventory.** The supplied CSV has 37
final-field rows and 36 distinct baseline_field values. The baseline data_source
appears twice because it produces two fields. Every other baseline field has
one final counterpart. Thus 36 − 1 + 2 = 37. Other renames and definition
changes matter even though they do not change the count.

| Baseline v1.1.1 | Final v1.1.2 | Recorded refinement |
|---|---|---|
| internal_identifier | document_label | Clarify the document-local label meaning. |
| geographic_granularity | geographic_level | Use clearer terminology for the represented geographic or reporting level. |
| measure_type | statistical_forms | Distinguish statistical form from the measured concept. |
| financing_source | funders | Clarify the funding-source meaning. |
| panel_title; variable_name | panel_titles; variable_names | Express repeatable flat values through plural names. |
| data_source | data_sources; attributions | Separate data origin from explicitly credited production. |

The module counts remain easy to inspect: Identity and discovery 5, Subject and
semantics 4, Temporal context 2, Spatial context 5, Measurement context 4,
Structural organization 3, Provenance and attribution 5, Project and operational
context 7, and Analytical and methodological context 2. Their total is 37.
Modules organize the specification; they do not make the record nested.

**Worked example: verify meanings alongside names.** The final definition of
project_name remains “The project, program, operation, or initiative associated
with the snapshot.” Its unchanged name does not imply that the narrower external
Project class was adopted. The final title definition remains “The primary title,
caption, or heading that identifies the data snapshot.” Relevant mappings and
guidance still clarify which resource the title names.

The final definition of time_period is “The date, interval, or source expression
describing when the represented data apply.” This is a further standards-informed
wording refinement. Keep it separate from both the original v1.1 definition and
the documentation clarification assessed in chapter 6. Final guidance preserves
the source expression and forbids unsupported temporal bounds or precision.

Every final field is optional. In the recorded specification, 0..1 denotes a
single optional text value and 0..* an optional ordered list of text values.
Missing metadata remains null. Preserve source order and remove only exact
duplicates within a field; a list of values does not establish associations
between values in different fields.

**Publication review.** The field inventory, exact definitions, examples,
authoritative references, human dispositions, and deferred requirements should
be mutually consistent. The chapter's worked comparisons cover a small selection
of standards terms; they do not replace the complete research specification or
certify every possible external mapping. Rechecking mutable term pages is part
of maintaining the crosswalk.

**Verification.** For the supplied exercise, check 37 unique final_field
values, all 36 distinct baseline fields, and the transcribed final cardinalities.
Explain the repeated baseline entry and trace every highlighted change to a
recorded decision. Review the selected definitions alongside the names, and
record the remaining publication and validation needs in the notes. These
outputs document the flat 37-field v1.1.2 endpoint without reconstructing its
complete specification.

For another collection, verify that the complete versioned specification
contains every field's definition and guidance, that accepted changes trace to
the decision record, and that deferred structures remain identified. State the
source evidence, reviewer, and evaluation boundary for the proposed publication.

**What this does not do.** Standards-informed alignment is not formal compliance
or measured interoperability. Reusing terminology differs from implementing a
standard's required representation and constraints; demonstrating conformance
would require a defined target and corresponding checks. The final flat schema
did not receive an additional independent held-out validation after refinement.

</Recipe>

## Expected artifact

The method ends with an operational specification suited to the artifact class,
a source-backed crosswalk, human refinement decisions, and explicit limitations.
Field counts, representation, and relevant standards follow that application's
evidence and uses. The study's sequence remains 70 candidate concepts, 41
reviewed concepts, 36 initial fields, 36 documentation-revised fields, and 37
final fields. Its empirical evidence concerns the selected Data Snapshots;
applying the reasoning elsewhere requires evidence for the new class.

## Implementation options

A readable specification and the combined review worksheet are sufficient for
the exercises. The historical standards review was interactive, human-adjudicated
work using the existing schema, validation evidence, and authoritative sources.
No fixed historical prompt, batch pipeline, or structured acceptance contract is
supplied here.

A downstream implementation can express types and constraints in JSON Schema,
Pydantic, or another machine-readable form. The manuscript's post-study diagnostic
also changed nesting, normalization, schema context, and reporting references.
Its comparison cannot isolate a representation-format effect or establish
extraction-performance superiority. That later implementation does not extend
the evidence boundary of the original held-out exercises.

## Common mistakes

- Similar names can be mistaken for semantic equivalence at the same resource level.
- A descriptive crosswalk category can be treated as a formally asserted mapping relation.
- An external vocabulary can narrow a local meaning or require unsupported normalization.
- Deferred objects can be presented as implemented relationships in a flat schema.
- The final standards-informed specification can be described as independently validated or formally compliant without supporting checks.

## Verification

- Worked comparisons identify authoritative definitions, local scope, source dates, and the recorded relationship judgment.
- Accepted, rejected, partially accepted, and deferred recommendations remain distinguishable from new teaching interpretations.
- Exact provenance definitions and printed sample values preserve the 36-to-37 transformation and attribution boundary.
- Inventory reconciliation and publication notes preserve the final flat specification's cardinalities and evaluation limits.
