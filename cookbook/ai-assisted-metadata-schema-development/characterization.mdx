# 1. Artifact Characterization and Sampling

**Question:** What must the schema describe, and which artifacts provide credible evidence of its requirements?

:::note[Structural draft]
This chapter establishes learning milestones and candidate recipes. Detailed
procedures, worked examples, executable exercises, and implementation checks
remain for later authoring. Intended results below describe planned recipes.
:::

## Rationale

An artifact is an object selected for description, such as a table, chart, or map. Defining the artifact class establishes what one metadata record describes. Several artifacts in the same document can have different subjects, periods, and populations.

A representative artifact collection should expose the diversity relevant to the intended use. Recording the selection preserves its rationale and a separate collection for later validation. Sampling requirements depend on diversity and scope.

## Target state

- The artifact class and descriptive scope are explicit.
- Development and held-out artifacts have stable identities and a documented selection rationale.
- The sample register states diversity, exclusions, and limits on generalization.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish an artifact from its parent document and analytical contents.
- Identify relevant diversity dimensions and explain snapshot-disjoint and document-disjoint selection.

## Prerequisite concepts

An intended descriptive use, candidate artifacts, and their source references are available. Metadata engineering concepts are introduced here.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Define the artifact class and inspect a small selection with a domain expert. |
| **AI-ready** | Record identities, selection criteria, provenance, and collection membership in a structured manifest. |
| **AI-native** | Use model assistance to characterize diversity while humans approve scope and sample boundaries. |

:::info[Running example]
The study used 210 development snapshots from three institutional corpora and 202 held-out snapshots. The Jordan chart, Afghanistan table, and Russian chart introduce selected examples. These three objects illustrate the evidence structure and do not replace the full study sample.
:::

## Implementation options

A spreadsheet, CSV manifest, or JSON register can preserve the selection. Model summaries can support review of diversity; the final scope and sample rationale remain human decisions.

## Common mistakes

- A document description can hide differences among its artifacts.
- A convenient sample can omit an important reporting style.
- Snapshot-disjoint samples can still share parent documents.
