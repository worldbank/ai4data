# 6. Schema Validation

**Question:** What does new artifact evidence reveal about missing concepts, unclear guidance, and representation limits?

## Rationale

Held-out schema validation examines operational fields and guidance against
artifacts excluded from initial schema development. It asks whether the
specification can describe new evidence adequately for its intended scope.
This differs from [chapter 4's concept coverage](./concept-refinement.mdx),
which classified development field profiles against reviewed concepts before
the operational fields existed. Here, the evidence is the held-out artifact,
its relevant context, and the frozen field definitions from
[chapter 5](./schema-design.mdx).

Choose the separation unit to match the intended claim. New artifacts from
familiar sources test a different boundary from artifacts drawn from new source
groups. Account for shared origins, versions, or containing objects and identify
any evidence reused while preparing the instrument. The snapshot and parent-
document checks below demonstrate this reasoning for the study's collection.

Choose assessment questions that could expose meaningful weaknesses in the
schema. One instrument or several may be appropriate; the exact historical pair
is not required. The study combined open residual discovery, which compares
possible descriptive requirements with existing fields, and a confirmatory
assessment focused on consequential omissions. Both produced candidate evidence.
Human review can justify new fields, clearer guidance, representation changes,
exclusion, or further investigation. The study's unchanged field inventory is
one outcome of that review. Another collection could justify new fields, and
stability alone does not establish completeness.

## Target state

- A validation specification identifies the frozen schema, collection, instruments, and calibration boundary.
- Evidence records preserve model proposals separately from human dispositions and accepted revisions.
- Documentation changes and structural limitations remain distinguishable, with conclusions bounded by the evaluated collection and instrument limitations.

## Learning objectives

- The practitioner can freeze and verify inputs without overstating their independence.
- The practitioner can inspect broad residual findings and consequential-gap assessments using their different contracts.
- The practitioner can adjudicate evidence and explain why a revision may preserve the field count.
- The practitioner can report limitations of calibration, sensitivity, and unaudited no-gap assessments.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 1 supplies the evidence-separation boundary. Chapter 5 supplies the
operational specification and the distinction between a value, a field, and a
representation. The practitioner can inspect JSON records and source references.
The exercises use retained excerpts and manual review. The reused manifest
checker needs Python 3.10 or newer; no model call or dataset download is required.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Reviewers inspect evidence and record attributable human decisions. |
| **AI-ready** | Frozen specifications, identities, output checks, and revision records support repeatable assessment. |
| **AI-native** | Complementary model instruments retain provenance, calibration evidence, and verification while humans decide changes. |

:::info[Running example]
**Recorded study result.** Both instruments assessed the same 202 held-out
snapshots. Validation 1 tested initial v1.1 and returned 2,876 observations and
578 candidate proposals. Human adjudication informed the documentation-only
v1.1.1 revision, which Validation 2 then assessed using a model-facing copy

snapshots. Human review accepted no new field; the inventory remained at 36.
**Teaching exercises.** Selected retained records and newly written review

## Recipes

<Recipe title="6.1 Review the schema and validation freeze" level="AI-ready" skills="Evaluation practitioner; metadata reviewer" time="30–45 minutes with prepared specifications and manifests">

**Result:** A freeze-review record identifies the assessed schema versions, collection boundary, execution settings, and independence limitations. A new assessment also retains its complete schema text and manifest.

A schema freeze prevents the target from changing during an assessment. It
must identify definitions and guidance as well as field names: two documents
with the same inventory can produce different judgments. A collection freeze
likewise records realized identities as well as the sampling procedure. Together,
they let a reviewer determine which specification met which evidence.

Before a full run, reviewers check assessment instructions and controls on
identified examples. These calibration examples remain distinguishable from
initial development and from artifacts untouched by calibration.

**Worked example: a filled freeze specification.** The following is a teaching
summary of the recorded inputs and execution. It is not an original protocol file.

| Freeze item | Recorded study specification |
|---|---|
| Initial target | Validation 1 used the complete v1.1 schema: 36 fields in nine modules. |
| Subsequent target | Validation 2 used documentation-revised v1.1.1, with the same 36 fields, through a model-facing copy omitting validation history and prior outcomes. |
| Assessed evidence | Both runs used the same 202 held-out snapshots, their identities, and available parent-document metadata. |
| Sampling boundary | Development had 210 snapshots; held-out had 202. Snapshot overlap was zero; 65 parent documents were shared. |
| Historical configuration | Both used GPT-5.5, medium reasoning, Flex service, prompt caching, an 8,000-token ceiling, and Pydantic Structured Outputs, one snapshot per request. |
| Completion boundary | Each full run retained 202 unique completed results whose filenames exactly match the held-out manifest. |
| Calibration boundary | Twelve of the 202 were reused for calibration. None belonged to initial schema development. Calibration/control outputs are excluded from full-run candidate totals. |

1. Compare the assessed versions in the supplied summary. For a new assessment, record the exact version and retain the complete schema text; a checksum can identify the frozen file. Preserve the assessed presentation separately when revision history or prior outcomes are removed from a model-facing copy.
2. Freeze the validation register, source versions, and allowed contextual inputs. Compare artifact identities and any source groups or containing objects relevant to the generalization claim. The study checked snapshots and parent documents. Keep calibration membership explicit.
3. State the objective and output contract of each instrument. Record model, prompt, response structure, and settings. Preserve full-run outputs separately from calibration and ablation controls.
4. Before interpreting results, check expected identities, duplicate and missing records, completion status, configuration consistency, and output-contract validity. Reconcile results against the frozen manifest. Directory traversal and response order alone do not prove membership.

The held-out allocation was 35 figures and 35 tables from each corpus except
Refugee Project Appraisal Documents, where only 27 eligible figures remained.
This produced 97 figures and 105 tables. The full manifests contain 132
development parents and 125 held-out parents. Shared parents can contribute
related terminology, document context, and presentation practices. The design
tests new snapshots within these reporting domains; it is not document-disjoint.

**Reused offline check.** Run the existing [manifest checker](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/check_manifest.py)
beside [source_images.csv](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/source_images.csv), as in chapter 1.

```bash
python check_manifest.py source_images.csv
```

```text
development: 6 snapshots, 6 parent documents
heldout: 2 snapshots, 2 parent documents
snapshot overlap: 0
shared parent documents: 0
```

The register is an eight-row teaching excerpt. This command demonstrates the
identity check and does not reconstruct the 210/202 manifests or their 65 shared
parents. The full-study comparison is separately recorded under
verification_derived_facts in [evidence.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/evidence.json).

**Verification.** Expect the four output lines and status 0 for the supplied
excerpt. For a new full assessment, verify every result against the actual
frozen manifest and schema text. The five initial field excerpts from chapter 5
cannot substitute for the complete historical model input. Preserve v1.1 and
v1.1.1 as different assessment inputs despite their equal field counts.

**What this does not do.** The checker does not evaluate semantic coverage or
establish document independence. The realized historical sample is reproducible
from its retained identities; rerunning the original unseeded random selection
need not reproduce it. A document-disjoint collection and an independent audit
would be proposed improvements requiring additional evidence.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.2 Inspect open residual-discovery findings" level="AI-native" skills="LLM practitioner; metadata reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for the selected observations and date-boundary review">

**Result:** An evidence-backed candidate list preserves supporting observations, exact existing fields, fit judgments, and issues requiring human adjudication.

Open residual discovery revisits what an artifact requires without treating
the frozen inventory as an exhaustive list of possible observations. It then
compares those observations with the specification. Unlike initial discovery
in chapter 2, this instrument receives the complete operational schema and
must explain fit before proposing a field. It remains broader than acceptance:
the prompt can surface plausible distinctions whose use-case value humans
subsequently reject.

**Input example: a field with an unclear date boundary.** The
documentation-revision example records artifact-date families such as
snapshot_date, snapshot_creation_date, and snapshot_production_date, alongside
parent-document publication-date proposals. The original time_period definition
was:

> The period or date range represented by the data.

1. For a new assessment, supply a reserved artifact in a representation appropriate to its modality, its identity, allowed context, and the complete frozen schema. State the class and descriptive scope. The historical request supplied one snapshot image and associated document metadata.
2. Inspect each observation's concept, evidence origin, closest existing fields, fit judgment, and rationale. In the supplied records, these appear in the observations array as observation_id, metadata_concept, evidence, evidence_source, closest_schema_fields, fit_status, and fit_rationale. Preserve its snapshot, document, or both labels when reviewing historical evidence.
3. Inspect candidate_new_fields. Trace each proposed name and definition through supporting_observation_ids to weak-fit or no-fit evidence in the same assessment. Read why_existing_fields_are_insufficient and operational_value as claims requiring review.
4. Retain covered evidence and genuine boundary questions. Group obvious aliases only as a labelled review aid. Frequency can direct inspection but cannot establish an accepted requirement.

| Historical fit_status | Meaning in the assessment |
|---|---|
| covered | Existing fields represent the concept without material loss. |
| weak_fit | A related field would distort, omit, or conflate important semantics. |
| no_fit | The concept is in scope and no field adequately represents it. |
| out_of_scope | The observation falls outside snapshot metadata, such as extraction output. |
| uncertain | Evidence or semantic interpretation is insufficient for a reliable decision. |

The historical prompt allowed document-administration proposals when they
materially enabled snapshot identification, retrieval, verification, or
interpretation. It did not already enforce every linked-document architecture
decision later applied by humans. Silently imposing Validation 2's tighter
parent-document boundary would misdescribe this first instrument.

**Worked example: reconcile observations and proposals.** The recorded fit
counts sum to 2,876: 2,275 covered, 185 weak_fit, 393 no_fit, 20 uncertain, and
three out_of_scope. There were 578 candidate objects using 101 exact proposed
names. Candidate count is its own output-array total. In this run, 185 + 393
also equals 578; the contract allows a proposal to cite multiple observations,
so that numerical equality is not a general one-to-one rule.

Human review manually grouped naming variants into 14 provisional families,
covering 518 candidate rows. The remaining 60 proposals and all 23 uncertain
or out-of-scope observations were also examined. These review families were
neither model output nor an accepted schema layer.

**Worked example: a documentation decision.** The adjudication report distinguishes a represented data period from an
artifact's creation or production date and a parent document's publication
date. The latter dates should not be placed in time_period solely because they
are available. A complete relevant footer statement can remain in
interpretive_note; parent-document administration remains on the linked record.

The accepted v1.1.1 documentation uses the following exact Markdown wording:

```markdown
The period or date range represented by the data.

This field describes **when the represented data apply**. It does not describe when the snapshot artifact or parent document was created, prepared, issued, published, revised, or retrieved. When an explicit artifact date appears only as part of a footer or provenance statement, preserve the complete statement in `interpretive_note` rather than treating the date as `time_period`.
```

This is a recorded field-level revision supported by the adjudication report.
It is not attributed to a uniquely decisive snapshot or to the South Sudan URL
candidate used below. The operational data-period concept already existed;
clarifying its boundary preserved the 36-field inventory.

**Verification.** Match both definitions to documentation-revision in
[evidence.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/evidence.json). Explain why creation and publication dates
are different roles from a represented data period. For each inspected candidate,
check its support identifiers, evidence source, exact closest fields, and
proposed semantic loss before entering a human decision.

**What this does not do.** A candidate is not a valid missing field by default.
Neither 578 proposals nor their recurrence establishes 578 requirements or
universal completeness. The historical run preserved successful results and
separate errors, resumed successful filenames, and used notebook-driven reruns
without an automatic retry policy. No live validation is executed here.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.3 Assess consequential omissions with a confirmatory instrument" level="AI-native" skills="Evaluation practitioner; domain reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for the paired example and instrument controls">

**Result:** A review of retained consequential-gap assessments identifies the evidence, closest fields, claimed material consequences, and uncertainty behind each selected proposal.

A confirmatory instrument tests a narrower question. It asks whether the best
representation permitted by existing fields loses reusable snapshot information
in a way that materially impairs interpretation or discovery. It does not
inventory covered concepts. Its lower candidate volume cannot by itself
establish greater accuracy, because the question and schema documentation
differ from open residual discovery.

The historical output has coverage_assessment and critical_or_possible_gaps.
A no_critical_gap_found result requires an empty array. A possible_gap result
requires one or more possible gaps and no critical gap. A critical_gap_found
result requires at least one critical gap. Each candidate records its identifier,
status, missing concept, proposed name, evidence, snapshot relevance, closest
fields, possible loss, material consequence, and uncertainty. The no-gap output
has no snapshot-level explanatory rationale.

**Input example: the same South Sudan composite.** The held-out artifact
protection_trends_paper_no_7_jan-mar_2016_final_figure_004.png contains a map,
two donut charts, and a trend chart with different subjects and periods.
The [externally linked image](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4data/data-snapshot/blob/626f3c54ba6d8f6e0ba0795f9e544e24b2650faf/snapshots/unhcr/protection_trends_paper_no_7_jan-mar_2016_final_figure_004.png) and
[source register](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/source_images.csv) retain its provenance. The
following retained text is sufficient for this exercise.

1. Freeze the schema version to be assessed and supply it with the reserved artifact representation and allowed context. If history is removed to reduce anchoring, preserve substantive meanings and guidance. The study's second instrument used a model-facing v1.1.1 copy after documentation revision; a new application need not reproduce that exact sequence.
2. Set the conditions for a consequential gap using the artifact scope and intended uses. The study required explicit evidence, reusable snapshot metadata, inadequate coverage, and material impact on interpretability or discoverability. Its possible-gap label retained uncertainty while still requiring plausible metadata and impact.
3. Compare candidate consequences with the application's stated uses and evidence rules. In this confirmatory instrument, convenience or an extra search facet alone did not meet the threshold, and the prompt forbade inventing a user workflow to inflate materiality. Preserve that distinction when interpreting its results.
4. Check assessment/array consistency, then inspect the underlying evidence. Preserve model assessments separately from human decisions; the historical model was expressly not asked to classify conceptual, documentation, or representation issues.

**Worked example.** The two instruments produced the following retained findings.

| Instrument and frozen target | Recorded result for this artifact | Interpretation for review |
|---|---|---|
| Validation 1, v1.1 | Observation obs_11 marked composite visualization types covered by visualization_type and panel_title. Observation obs_16 marked a parent retrieval URL no_fit and supported source_document_url. | The model distinguished descriptive content already covered from a potentially useful parent-document locator. |
| Validation 2, model-facing v1.1.1 | gap_001 proposed panel_metadata_mapping as possible, with an interpretability consequence. | The concern was preserving which measured concept, period, and visualization type belongs to each panel. |

The first instrument found panel titles and visual types expressible. The
second questioned the associations among those values. Those findings address
different aspects of coverage; they do not demonstrate a direct contradiction
or establish which instrument is more sensitive. Both assessed the same artifact with available parent-document context. Their
prompts and schema documentation differed. Neither
model accepted a field.

**Recorded full-run result.** There were 197 no_critical_gap_found assessments,
five possible_gap assessments, and zero critical_gap_found assessments.
Thus 197 + 5 = 202 snapshots. The five positive snapshots yielded six possible
gap records: one project table produced two, and the other four produced one
each. These are artifact-level and gap-level counts with different denominators.

**Calibration and sensitivity limits.** An ablation is a control that deliberately
removes a known field to test whether the instrument notices the omission.
The retained Validation 2 sequence was:

| Control or calibration | Recorded outcome | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Twelve snapshots with canonical v1.1.1 including prior outcomes | All twelve returned no-gap assessments. | Prior-outcome language created an avoidable anchoring risk. |
| The same twelve with the model-facing copy | Eleven no-gap assessments and one possible component-relationship gap. | The presentation change was checked while preserving substantive schema content. |
| Two title-ablation calls | Both returned no-gap assessments after title was removed. | The instrument failed the intended title-sensitivity signal. |
| One source-and-note ablation | A critical underlying-data-source gap was returned after data_source and interpretive_note were removed. | The instrument detected at least this explicit provenance omission. |

All these calibration/control identities belonged to the same twelve snapshots.
The full run restored the complete 36-field model-facing specification.
Calibration results are excluded from the six substantive candidates. The
remaining 190 snapshots were untouched by this calibration, but the full 202
had already served as Validation 1 evidence. This is not a fresh independent
replication or a collection wholly untouched by instrument development.

Human review inspected all six surfaced gaps. The 197 no-gap assessments had
no independent human false-negative audit. The successful provenance control
does not repair the failed title control or estimate general recall. A no-gap
response is a model assessment under this instrument. It does not prove that
every consequential omission was absent.

**Verification.** Reconcile snapshot and gap counts separately. Compare the
South Sudan records under complementary-validation and flat-representation-limits
in [evidence.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/evidence.json). Explain the different questions, schema
presentations, and remaining uncertainty before describing the findings as
complementary evidence.

**What this does not do.** The result does not measure a false-negative rate,
prove universal saturation, establish superiority over other methodologies, or
provide an independently audited completeness score. The control removing
data_source and interpretive_note is a methodological test. The complete
schema retained those fields.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.4 Adjudicate findings and record justified revisions" level="Foundational" skills="Domain expert; metadata designer" time="60–90 minutes for the selected findings and decision worksheet">

**Result:** A human-reviewed decision record traces evidence to documentation changes, retained coverage, exclusions, unresolved questions, or a representation backlog.

Adjudication turns a model's proposed distinction into a decision about the
application's schema. Reviewers must evaluate evidence, scope, existing coverage,
materiality, and operational value before selecting an action. A finding can
be real while its proposed flat field is an unsuitable response. The decision
record preserves that distinction so that later work does not rediscover the
same issue or mistake a deferred design for an implemented solution.

**Input example: table hierarchy.** The Diffa table is
rapport_du_monitoring_de_protection_de_la_region_de_diffa_du_mois_de_octobre_2022_ciaud_niger_table_002.png.
Its retained assessment identifies spanning headers that group incident
subtypes beneath broader categories. The [externally linked table](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4data/data-snapshot/blob/626f3c54ba6d8f6e0ba0795f9e544e24b2650faf/snapshots/unhcr/rapport_du_monitoring_de_protection_de_la_region_de_diffa_du_mois_de_octobre_2022_ciaud_niger_table_002.png) remains available through the pinned dataset record. Existing row, column, and category fields
can retain labels while losing the parent-child relationship. This exercise
uses that recorded structural account and does not translate the French labels
or reconstruct the visibly clipped upper headers. The [source register](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/source_images.csv)
keeps the image externally linked and records that limitation.

1. Save a working copy of the [gap-review template](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/gap_review_template.csv). Identify the artifact, schema version, candidate, evidence, and closest fields. The template calls its artifact column snapshot_id for this case; another review should identify its own artifact unit. Pair local candidate identifiers with artifact identities to avoid ambiguity.
2. Inspect source evidence and compare its meaning with the best use of existing fields. Ask whether the requirement describes the artifact, contextual information, contents outside the agreed scope, or a relationship the representation loses. Judge coverage using the application's scope and its own field definitions.
3. Classify the issue using the teaching framework below, then document material_impact, decision, decision_reason, revision_or_deferral, and the actual reviewer for a new review. Preserve the source report's terminology when transcribing a historical disposition.
4. Check that a proposed revision addresses the demonstrated issue. Version accepted field, guidance, or representation changes and compare the specification before and after review. Keep deferred requirements explicit. An unresolved case remains unresolved until additional evidence supports a decision.

| Teaching issue category | Review question and possible response |
|---|---|
| Missing concept or field | Does necessary, reusable, in-scope information remain inadequately covered? Consider a field only after verifying evidence and operational need. |
| Documentation ambiguity | Does an existing field cover the concept but lack clear scope or annotation rules? Clarify and version the guidance. |
| Representation limitation | Are the concepts present but relationships lost? Record the required association or hierarchy and assess a structural design separately. |
| Unsupported, excluded, or insufficiently material | Does evidence, scope, or material consequence fail the review? Reject or exclude with an attributable reason. |
| Unresolved | What additional evidence or expert judgment is needed? Defer the decision explicitly. |

These categories are a practical teaching framework. Validation 2 did not ask
the model to produce them, and the reports used their own disposition wording.
The following rows are newly written teaching transcriptions of recorded human
outcomes. They are not an original item-by-item adjudication ledger.

The two South Sudan rows trace separate assessments of the same artifact,
each with its own candidate finding and human interpretation. The model evidence
comes from complementary-validation and flat-representation-limits. The human
columns are teaching transcriptions of the retained adjudication, separate from
the model judgments.

| Instrument and target | Candidate finding | Human interpretation | Actual outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Validation 1, v1.1; documentation-revision | Artifact-date families exposed the time_period boundary. | Data periods differ from artifact creation and parent publication dates. | Clarify guidance in v1.1.1; retain 36 fields and keep document administration on the linked record. |
| Validation 1, v1.1; South Sudan obs_16 | no_fit parent retrieval information supported source_document_url. | Retrieval metadata belongs to the linked parent-document record in this application. | Exclude the proposed snapshot-level field; no new snapshot field accepted. |
| Validation 2, model-facing v1.1.1; South Sudan gap_001 | possible panel_metadata_mapping gap with an interpretability consequence. | Relevant concepts exist, but flat values cannot preserve which variable, period, and visual type belong to each panel. | Defer the representation requirement to future v1.2 design; add no top-level flat field. |
| Validation 2, model-facing v1.1.1; Diffa gap_001 | possible column_group_hierarchy gap. | Category and dimension fields lose parent-child header relationships. | Defer the structural requirement; add no new flat field. |

**Worked example: document a clarification.** In a worksheet row for the date
family, leave snapshot_id empty and explain that the evidence is report-level.
Use a clearly labelled teaching family reference in candidate_id; do not invent
a historical candidate identifier or decisive artifact. Reference
documentation-revision, record the v1.1 to v1.1.1 change, and compare the exact
wording in recipe 6.2. The report also discussed future design work. Only
changes present in the retained revision count as implemented changes.

**Worked example: panel relationships.** For the South Sudan figure, existing
fields can name titles, variables, periods, and visualization types. Listing
those values does not say which period and variable apply to a particular
panel. Record that association requirement and the reported representation-gap
disposition. The historical report described deferral to future v1.2 design;
it did not establish an implemented normalized schema.

Human review of Validation 2 rejected or excluded four proposals and deferred
two representation issues. The other proposed names were
data_collection_responsibility, data_collection_frequency, uncertainty_indicator,
and visual_annotation. Their recorded problems concerned specialized operational
table contents, unlabeled uncertainty-like marks, or a document-reading cue
without sufficient reusable snapshot-level value. Rarity alone was not the
rejection criterion. Neither validation accepted an additional metadata field.

**Verification.** A second reviewer can follow every selected disposition from
artifact or report reference to the relevant definition, decision, and concrete
revision or deferral. Historical reviewer identities are not supplied in these
teaching rows; a new review must record its actual participants. Confirm that
v1.1 and v1.1.1 contain the same 36 field names and that representation backlog
items are not claimed as implemented fixes.

**What this does not do.** Stable field count does not mean every definition
was adequate or every relationship expressible. These decisions support a
bounded claim about the tested collection and intended application. The later
standards-informed 37-field endpoint belongs to [chapter 7](./standards-refinement.mdx)
and did not receive a further independent held-out assessment in this study.

</Recipe>

## Expected artifact

Validation produces evidence-backed assessments, human dispositions, and a
versioned specification or explicit requirements for further work. Accepted
changes depend on the findings and application. In the study, the implemented
revision was documentation-only v1.1.1 with 36 fields; representation requirements
remained deferred. These outputs carry forward into standards
comparison without converting proposals or an unchanged inventory into claims
of complete coverage.

## Common mistakes

- Candidate objects, unique proposed names, affected snapshots, and accepted fields can be reported as interchangeable counts.
- Equal field inventories can obscure differences in schema definitions and model-facing presentation.
- Complementary assessments of the same collection can be described as independent replications.
- A no-gap model response can be mistaken for an independent human completeness audit.
- A representation limitation can be treated as resolved by adding an isolated flat field.

## Verification

- The schema text, manifest identities, parent overlap, and twelve-snapshot calibration boundary are explicit.
- Observation, proposal, assessment, and gap counts reconcile with the retained results and their separate units.
- Exact date-field wording and selected human dispositions preserve the difference between implemented documentation and deferred structure.
- Conclusions retain the failed title control, successful bounded provenance control, absence of a no-gap audit, and unchanged 36-field inventory.
