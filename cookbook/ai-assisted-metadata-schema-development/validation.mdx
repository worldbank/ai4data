# 6. Schema Validation

**Question:** What does new artifact evidence reveal about missing concepts, unclear guidance, and representation limits?

:::note[Structural draft]
This chapter establishes learning milestones and candidate recipes. Detailed
procedures, worked examples, executable exercises, and implementation checks
remain for later authoring. Intended results below describe planned recipes.
:::

## Rationale

Held-out validation assesses an operational schema against artifacts excluded from initial development. Open residual discovery explores omissions. A confirmatory instrument asks whether an omission materially impairs interpretation or discovery.

Human adjudication determines the response to each finding. A candidate can expose a missing field, unclear guidance, a representation limitation, or an out-of-scope detail. A stable field inventory can coexist with useful revisions.

## Target state

- The schema and validation collection have frozen identities.
- Candidate evidence is separated from human decisions and revisions.
- Conclusions state the tested scope and instrument limitations.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish development concept coverage from held-out schema validation.
- Compare complementary instruments and classify field, documentation, representation, scope, and uncertainty issues.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 5 provides the frozen initial schema. Chapter 1 supplies the held-out boundary. The practitioner understands that a proposed gap does not establish a required field.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Review selected artifacts, findings, and human dispositions. |
| **AI-ready** | Freeze instrument inputs and maintain structured evidence, decisions, and revisions. |
| **AI-native** | Use bounded model-assisted open and confirmatory assessment with calibration, integrity checks, and human adjudication. |

:::info[Running example]
Both exercises used the same 202 held-out snapshots. Validation 1 produced 578 proposals. Validation 2 produced six possible gaps across five snapshots. Human review accepted no new field. The time_period clarification, South Sudan panel associations, and Diffa header hierarchy illustrate different outcomes.
:::

## Expected artifact

Validation evidence, human dispositions, a documentation-revised schema, and a representation backlog will support standards-informed refinement.

## Implementation options

Open and confirmatory instruments answer complementary questions. Human source inspection remains necessary. An independent audit of no-gap cases could strengthen a future evaluation; the study did not include that audit.

## Common mistakes

- Two instruments on the same collection can be described as independent replications.
- An unchanged field inventory can be mistaken for an absence of useful findings.
- No-gap assessments can be used to claim an unmeasured false-negative rate.
