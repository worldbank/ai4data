# 5. Operational Metadata Schema Design

**Question:** How do meaningful concepts become fields that people can use consistently?

## Rationale

A concept inventory establishes meaningful distinctions. An operational metadata
schema specifies the fields used to describe artifacts, including their names,
definitions, evidence rules, examples, and treatment of unavailable information.
Its design must support consistent annotation, interpretation, and discovery
with an annotation and maintenance effort that the intended application can sustain.

The transition requires human decisions beyond semantic consolidation. Two
concepts may be distinct yet offer little benefit as separate fields. A
previously consolidated distinction may become useful when annotating complex
artifacts. The schema's representation also determines which relationships a
record can express. Reviewing these choices precedes freezing the initial
specification for [held-out validation](./validation.mdx).

## Target state

- Field decisions state their operational purpose and relationship to reviewed concepts.
- A versioned specification defines field meanings, applicability, examples, and evidence boundaries.
- A development-example review records ambiguities and representation questions before the initial schema is frozen.

## Learning objectives

- The practitioner can justify keeping, merging, removing, renaming, or restoring a field.
- The practitioner can distinguish a concept, field definition, annotation rule, value, and record.
- The practitioner can review selected annotations without claiming a new historical experiment or incorporating later validation revisions.

## Prerequisite concepts

[Chapter 4](./concept-refinement.mdx) supplies the 41 reviewed concepts and their
original coverage evidence. The intended annotation and retrieval uses are
explicit. A domain reviewer can inspect source evidence and compare plausible
interpretations. The exercises use document review and a blank CSV worksheet;
they require no code execution, model call, or dataset download.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Human-readable definitions and decision reasons establish an annotatable specification. |
| **AI-ready** | Versioned field specifications, source references, and review records support repeatable checks. |
| **AI-native** | Model-assisted ambiguity checks retain evidence and verification while humans approve field and representation decisions. |

## Recipes

<Recipe title="5.1 Translate concepts into operational field decisions" level="Foundational" skills="Metadata designer; domain reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for the representative decisions">

**Result:** A field-decision record explains which distinctions receive independent fields and why those choices support the intended annotation and retrieval tasks.

The number of fields cannot be derived by copying concept names. An independent
field needs an interpretable purpose, evidence that annotators can recognize,
and enough downstream value to justify its maintenance. Combining fields can
reduce duplicate annotation; restoring a distinction can make a composite
artifact easier to describe. Both choices require an operational rationale.

1. Start with the reviewed meanings and original support evidence. For each proposed independent field, identify its annotation purpose, useful retrieval behavior, and overlap with other fields.
2. Compare keeping the distinction separately with expressing its useful information in an existing field. Consider consistency, interpretability, redundancy, maintenance effort, and representation constraints. Record the benefit and any loss.
3. Preserve a concept-to-field note for each demonstrated change. Distinguish a rename from a change in meaning. State when a field restores a distinction that the concept inventory did not retain separately.
4. Freeze the selected field names and specification version before validation. Retain unresolved operational issues as review questions, with the evidence needed to revisit them.

**Worked example.** The paper's Table 14 and operational-panel-title in
[evidence.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/evidence.json) record the following representative design
decisions. These examples do not enumerate every transition from 41 concepts
to 36 fields.

| Reviewed meaning or earlier distinction | Recorded operational decision | Why the decision matters |
|---|---|---|
| Related indicator meanings, including series_labels | Consolidate series_labels into indicator_name during design. | Separate fields added little annotation or retrieval value for the intended use. |
| calculation_method | Consolidate into analysis_method. | A broader field retained useful methodological information without parallel vocabulary. |
| Panel-level titles | Restore panel_title as an independent field. | Multi-panel figures benefit from separately annotatable headings. |
| indicator_definition | Remove as an independent field. | Its operational value did not warrant the additional field. |
| financial_metric | Rename to financial_measure. | The label improves clarity and consistency in the specification. |

The indicator terminology crosses a stage boundary. The reviewed concept is
indicator_name, and the recorded design discussion uses that name for the
series-label consolidation. The actual initial v1.1 specification calls the
operational field **variable_name**. Its definition covers variables, indicators,
metrics, and measured concepts. Use that version's field name when writing an
operational record; a conceptual label is not automatically a schema key.

The soybean figure, document_12916918_figure_012.png, has a recorded panel_titles
observation: “Monthly Price; Monthly Returns (% change) and KL (2000) test
detected breaks.” The original coverage mapping assigned that profile to
not_in_ontology. The reviewed inventory had no independent panel_title target.
The paper describes the earlier conceptual consolidation under title and the
subsequent operational restoration. These are distinct records of different
stages. The original classifier did not use a panel_title target.

The later mapping assigns panel_titles to panel_title. It is subsequent
context for the operational distinction. The soybean example demonstrates the
need without establishing that this image was the uniquely decisive historical
case. No exact edit date or undocumented discussion is inferred.

**Verification.** A reviewer can explain the purpose of each demonstrated
change and trace it to the evidence. Confirm that v1.1 has variable_name and
panel_title, while the original 41-concept inventory has indicator_name and no
independent panel_title. Explain why preserving a field for panel headings does
not require restoring every low-frequency concept as a field.

**What this does not do.** The selected record does not reconstruct a complete
concept-to-field crosswalk or calculate the field count by subtracting five
concepts. The initial 36-field specification is a recorded human design output;
later validation and standards changes remain outside this recipe.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="5.2 Write field definitions and annotation guidance" level="Foundational" skills="Metadata designer; annotation reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for five contrasting fields">

**Result:** A field-specification worksheet separates source definitions from teaching guidance and documents scope, evidence, examples, related fields, and missing-value behavior.

A concept definition states a shared meaning. An operational field definition
states what belongs in a specific schema field. Annotation guidance explains
how to apply that definition to evidence. An observed value instantiates it for
one artifact. A complete metadata record brings the applicable fields together
for that identified artifact. Reviewing a few fields is useful without claiming
to have produced or verified a complete record.

1. Identify the schema version and transcribe the selected definitions faithfully. Keep proposed clarifications in a separate worksheet column so that a later rule cannot silently replace the initial definition.
2. State each field's purpose, applicability, acceptable evidence, and boundary with related fields. Use both a fitting example and a case that should remain unpopulated. Do not turn a model's uncertainty phrase into a factual value.
3. Apply the initial general rules: all fields are optional, values need explicit support, source-document context is used where permitted, and missing metadata is represented as null. Record missing and non-applicable reasons separately during review if the schema itself does not distinguish them.
4. Ask another reviewer to interpret the same examples from the specification. Record disagreements and proposed guidance for testing. A new clarification remains a proposal until it is approved and versioned.

The [initial field excerpts](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/initial_field_specifications.md) reproduce
five definitions and their illustrative examples from v1.1. They are a selected
transcription of the initial schema. The [field-specification template](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/field_specification_template.csv)
is a new blank teaching worksheet; save a working copy outside the supplied
evidence files.

| Initial v1.1 field | Exact definition, including field-specific guidance where present |
|---|---|
| title | The primary title, caption, or heading that identifies the data snapshot. |
| panel_title | The title or heading of an individual panel within a multi-panel snapshot. Populate only when panel titles are explicitly present. |
| variable_name | The primary variable, indicator, metric, or measured concept represented by the snapshot. |
| time_period | The period or date range represented by the data. |
| interpretive_note | Explanatory notes, caveats, assumptions, footnotes, or methodological remarks explicitly provided within the snapshot to aid interpretation. Populate only when such notes are explicitly present. |

**Worked example: separate a title from panel headings.** The soybean discovery
record identifies the overall figure title as “Soybeans” and separately records
the panel headings quoted in recipe 5.1. A newly constructed v1.1 teaching
application puts the overall heading in title and the explicit panel headings
in panel_title. It uses variable_name for what the panels measure. This shows
field roles; it does not establish new historical annotation output or an
association structure linking each panel to all its properties.

**Worked example: preserve supported text.** The Lebanon discovery record's
title is “Figure 8: Reasons for not accessing ANC (n=44).” A teaching application
can preserve that complete title in title without adding sample_size. Where
an explicit sample-size statement serves as an explanatory note, a reviewer can
consider it under interpretive_note's existing definition. That is an
application of note guidance. It leaves the earlier residual assignment unchanged.
Individual quantitative results remain outside the metadata record.

For the Afghanistan table, the original response says its unit is “Not
identifiable from this snapshot.” That phrase reports an observation limit.
In a v1.1 teaching record, unit_of_measure would remain null until explicit
support is available. The absence of a unit does not authorize inferring a
currency from the country's identity or the table's subject.

The Afghanistan table also illustrates the parent-document boundary. Its parent
is “Fragility and Population Movement in Afghanistan,” while the snapshot has
the income-and-remittances title shown in chapter 2. The initial schema provides
source_document_title for the parent reference. That does not make the parent's
administrative metadata values for the artifact's descriptive fields. The
individual income amounts remain analytical contents outside this specification.

**Version check.** The initial time_period definition is exactly “The period
or date range represented by the data.” It does not include the extended
documentation introduced after validation. Likewise, the initial provenance
field is data_source; the later standards-informed split is not part of this
chapter's specification. Candidate discovery's allowance for labelled inference
also does not override v1.1's operational rule against unsupported values.

**Verification.** The five worksheet definitions match the initial excerpts.
Any newly authored inclusion or exclusion guidance is labelled as teaching
guidance. Reviewers distinguish title, panel_title, and variable_name, preserve
the complete Lebanon title, and leave an unsupported Afghanistan unit null.
No later time-period wording is attributed to v1.1.

**What this does not do.** The worksheet is not a complete 36-field transcription,
a type or cardinality schema, or a production extractor. The source's examples
illustrate field meanings and are not all empirical annotations. New teaching
applications do not certify the correctness of discovery outputs.

<Recipe title="5.3 Review the schema against representative examples" level="AI-ready" skills="Annotation practitioner; independent domain reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for two development artifacts">

**Result:** An initial review record identifies evidence-supported field applications, missing or non-applicable values, interpretive disagreements, and questions to test after freezing the schema.

Applying the specification to contrasting development artifacts can reveal
unclear boundaries before a validation run. The exercise asks whether two
reviewers can use the fields consistently and whether the chosen representation
can express the required information. It is a design review using development
evidence. It does not claim an independent test of the schema's adequacy.

1. Select contrasting development artifacts and retain their identities from source_images.csv. Use the Afghanistan table and soybean figure here. Leave held-out identities unused during this review.
2. Independently apply the selected v1.1 fields using the excerpted definitions and attributable evidence. For each populated value, identify its source. Leave unsupported fields null and record a separate review reason.
3. Compare results. Distinguish unavailable information from a field that does not apply. A null may encode both cases in the initial record, so retain their reasons in the review worksheet. Record overlaps, disagreements, and representation questions without silently changing the specification.
4. Resolve or explicitly defer material issues, identify the reviewer and evidence, and freeze a named specification version with its field inventory. Send the initial schema and unresolved test questions to [chapter 6](./validation.mdx).

**Worked example.** The following is a newly constructed, partial teaching
review grounded in the development observations and source register. These
rows are not complete records or a reported historical validation exercise.

| Artifact and selected field | Teaching application | Review reason |
|---|---|---|
| Afghanistan table: title | Monthly labor income in Afghanistan and remittances from abroad | The recorded table-title observation supplies the explicit heading. |
| Afghanistan table: unit_of_measure | null | The discovery record explicitly reports that the unit is not identifiable. This is unavailable evidence. |
| Afghanistan table: panel_title | null in this illustration | The retained text supplies a table title and no explicit panel headings. Confirm applicability from the artifact; missing excerpt evidence alone cannot prove inapplicability. |
| Soybean figure: title | Soybeans | The recorded overall heading identifies the figure. |
| Soybean figure: panel_title | Monthly Price; Monthly Returns (% change) and KL (2000) test detected breaks | The recorded panel_titles observation supplies explicit headings for a multi-panel figure. |

For the soybean figure, ask whether listing headings is sufficient for the
intended use, or whether a downstream application needs an explicit association
between each panel and its properties. The initial specification is flat. A
list of multiple values alone does not define those associations. Record the
question and its material consequence for testing; this teaching review does
not report a later held-out finding or add a nested structure.

**Verification.** Check the table sum against the recorded 36-field inventory.
A review row identifies the artifact, field, schema version, evidence, reviewer,
issue, and disposition. The supplied illustration establishes examples and
expected reasoning; a new application's completed review must supply its actual
reviewer and decisions. Confirm that any unresolved ambiguity is retained as a
test question before the schema is frozen.

**What this does not do.** This development review does not estimate annotation
agreement, measure performance on unseen artifacts, or establish universal
coverage. No historical review session or decision date is reconstructed.
Validation-driven documentation and the later 37-field endpoint remain outside
the initial design boundary.

## Expected artifact

The initial operational schema has 36 fields, grouped into nine readable
modules, with definitions and evidence rules. Representative design decisions
and an explicit review record make the freeze interpretable. The next stage
evaluates that specification on held-out artifacts; it does not silently replace
the initial design with a later version.

## Implementation options

A readable specification and CSV worksheet support the manual exercises.
Machine-readable validation can check a future record's structure once types
and cardinalities have been decided. It cannot establish that a value is
source-supported or semantically appropriate. A richer representation needs a
documented requirement and a separate design decision. Neither a general schema
editor nor a live extraction service is needed for this chapter.

## Common mistakes

- A concept inventory can be copied into fields without reviewing annotation purpose or practical value.
- The conceptual name indicator_name can be mistaken for the initial schema's variable_name key.
- The later panel mapping can be treated as evidence that the original assessment included panel_title.
- Missing excerpt evidence can be mistaken for proof that a field is inapplicable.
- Later documentation or standards changes can silently replace the initial specification.

## Verification

- Representative decisions explain the operational benefit and preserve the 41-concept to 36-field boundary.
- Selected definitions match v1.1, and added teaching guidance remains distinguishable from source wording.
- Partial annotations retain evidence and missing-value reasons without claiming complete historical records.
- The initial freeze, nine modules, and 36 fields remain separate from held-out findings and later schema versions.
