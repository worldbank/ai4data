# 5. Operational Metadata Schema Design

**Question:** How do meaningful concepts become fields that people can use consistently?

:::note[Structural draft]
This chapter establishes learning milestones and candidate recipes. Detailed
procedures, worked examples, executable exercises, and implementation checks
remain for later authoring. Intended results below describe planned recipes.
:::

## Rationale

An operational metadata schema specifies fields that people can populate consistently. Its design balances conceptual distinctions with annotation effort, intended use, and maintainability. A retained concept does not automatically require a separate field.

Operational design can combine concepts, remove a costly distinction, or restore a practically useful field. Definitions, evidence rules, examples, and scope notes make these choices usable and reviewable.

## Target state

- Operational field decisions have explicit reasons.
- The specification supplies definitions, examples, and evidence boundaries.
- The initial schema remains distinguishable from later documentation and standards revisions.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish a concept inventory from an operational schema.
- Explain operational tradeoffs and assess whether the representation preserves the intended information.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 4 provides refined concepts and coverage evidence. Intended annotation and retrieval uses are explicit, and a human reviewer can judge the value of independent fields.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write field definitions and operational decision reasons. |
| **AI-ready** | Maintain a versioned specification with evidence rules, examples, and reviewable decisions. |
| **AI-native** | Use model assistance to challenge ambiguity while humans approve fields, definitions, and representation. |

## Expected artifact

An initial operational schema and field-decision record will be frozen for held-out validation.

## Implementation options

A flat specification supports human inspection. A minimal machine-readable illustration may clarify types or cardinalities during later authoring. Richer structures require a justified representation need.

## Common mistakes

- Concepts can be copied into fields without operational review.
- A definition can leave document-context and missing-value boundaries unclear.
- Extra flat fields can be proposed for information that requires relationships.
