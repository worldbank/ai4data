# Practical Guide to AI-Assisted Metadata Schema Development

**A cookbook for technical practitioners familiar with Python, APIs, and LLMs who want to learn metadata schema development.** Version 0.1 (working draft), November 2026.

:::note[Working draft]
This guide is a structural draft from the AI for Data – Data for AI program.
Its overview, learning milestones, candidate recipes, and prepared evidence
are available for review. Full procedures and executable exercises remain to
be authored. It does not represent official World Bank Group guidance or policy.
Corrections are welcome through [GitHub issues](https://github.com/worldbank/ai4data/issues);
the [contributing page](./contributing.md) describes the review expectations.
:::

An emerging artifact class can require metadata before its descriptive
requirements are fully understood. A table, chart, or map may need to be found
and interpreted independently of its parent document. Developing its schema
therefore begins with evidence about what the artifacts represent and the uses
their descriptions must support.

This cookbook develops four connected practices. Independent discovery proposes
potential metadata requirements from each artifact. Exact-name field profiles
preserve accumulated evidence, and concept synthesis and expert refinement
establish shared meanings. Operational schema design translates concepts into
usable fields and guidance. Held-out validation and standards-informed
refinement test that specification and clarify its terminology and boundaries.
Humans retain authority over scope, field design, and accepted changes.

The running example is the Data Snapshot study by Dy & Solatorio (2026).
Its evidence connects observations, profiles, concepts, schema decisions,
validation findings, and standards mappings. The methodology is intended for
adaptation to other artifact classes; the reported empirical evaluation concerns
the studied Data Snapshot collections. The [standards page](./standards.md)
identifies the references compared in the study.

## Purpose and learning outcomes

- The practitioner can distinguish a metadata observation, metadata field profile, canonical metadata concept, concept inventory, and operational metadata schema.
- The practitioner can trace an example from artifact evidence to a human-approved field decision.
- The practitioner can distinguish concept coverage from held-out validation and adjudicate documentation and representation issues.
- The practitioner can use a standards crosswalk while preserving artifact-derived requirements.

## Prerequisites

The foundational path assumes familiarity with Python, APIs, and LLMs, access
to representative artifacts, and a reviewer who understands their domain.
Metadata engineering concepts are introduced when needed. The prepared evidence
can be inspected without an API key or a new experiment.

The AI-ready path adds structured records, stable identities, and deterministic
checks. The AI-native path adds bounded model assistance with recorded inputs,
settings, evidence, and human decisions. Later live recipes will state their
dependencies and execution limits when implemented.

## First steps

1. Read [chapter 1](./characterization.mdx) to identify the artifact class and descriptive boundary. Allow about 20 minutes for the draft and evidence inspection.
2. Inspect naming in [chapter 2](./discovery.mdx) and profiling in [chapter 3](./concept-synthesis.mdx). Allow about 30 minutes; executable exercises are pending.
3. Follow original concept assessment into operational design in [chapters 4](./concept-refinement.mdx) and [5](./schema-design.mdx). Allow about 30 minutes for the selected decisions.
4. Compare validation in [chapter 6](./validation.mdx) and provenance refinement in [chapter 7](./standards-refinement.mdx). Allow about 30 minutes for inspection.

These are indicative reading estimates. Applying the methodology to a new
collection requires sampling, domain review, and additional work.
The [self-assessment](./start-here.mdx) provides one checkpoint per chapter.

## Maturity levels

An organization can reach different levels in different chapters. Human review
remains necessary at every level. Greater automation does not grant authority
to accept schema changes.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | The practice exists in a form a person can use and a crawler can read. Practitioners inspect evidence and record human decisions. |
| **AI-ready** | The practice is structured, identified, and accessible through documented interfaces. Observations, mappings, and decisions support repeatable checks. |
| **AI-native** | AI-oriented methods have provenance and verification built in. Bounded model assistance supports discovery, comparison, and assessment under human adjudication. |

## Running example

The research treated Data Snapshots as independent analytical information
objects. It processed 210 development snapshots from three institutional corpora,
producing 3,041 metadata observations and 833 field profiles. Synthesis proposed
70 concepts; expert refinement retained 41. Operational design produced an
initial 36-field schema. Two complementary exercises used the same 202 held-out
snapshots. Documentation refinement retained 36 fields; standards-informed
refinement produced a final 37-field flat specification.

Ten prepared examples form a continuous learning journey. The Jordan chart,
Afghanistan table, and Russian chart introduce discovery and aggregation.
Title profiles, concept decisions, and rare or residual assignments connect
synthesis to refinement. The soybean figure illustrates the later restoration
of panel_title. The South Sudan composite and Diffa table expose representation
limits during validation. The Afghanistan table returns for the distinction
between data sources and attribution.

The [public dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4data/data-snapshot)
contains source images and document metadata. The prepared register pins the
selected records to dataset revision
626f3c54ba6d8f6e0ba0795f9e544e24b2650faf. Images remain externally linked
because individual redistribution rights have not been established.

## Scope of the examples

Recorded study results, selected excerpts, preparation-time deterministic
exercises, and proposed extensions retain distinct labels. A simplified example
does not claim an independent execution of the study. The study sample size
is not a universal minimum.

Chapter 4 uses the original mapping against 41 concepts. Chapter 5 introduces
the subsequent restoration of panel_title during operational design. Later
mapping edits are kept separate from the original assessment. Both validations
used the same snapshot-disjoint collection, which shares some parent documents
with development. Neither accepted a new field after human review.
The final 37-field specification followed validation and did not receive a
further independent held-out assessment in the reported study.

The evidence supports bounded conclusions for the tested collections. It does
not establish universal generalizability, superiority over alternatives, or a
false-negative rate. Production metadata extraction, document layout detection,
formal ontology construction, and knowledge graph infrastructure are outside
this guide. Post-study machine-readable implementation remains separate from
the research endpoint.
