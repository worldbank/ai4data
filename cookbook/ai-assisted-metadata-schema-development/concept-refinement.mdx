# 4. Expert Concept Refinement and Assessment

**Question:** Which concepts should remain, and how does the development evidence support them?

:::note[Structural draft]
This chapter establishes learning milestones and candidate recipes. Detailed
procedures, worked examples, executable exercises, and implementation checks
remain for later authoring. Intended results below describe planned recipes.
:::

## Rationale

A concept inventory records shared descriptive meanings before operational fields are designed. Human refinement determines equivalence, scope, clarity, and conceptual boundaries. Frequency and similarity supply evidence for these decisions.

Concept coverage assessment assigns development field profiles to reviewed concepts or a residual category. The assignments expose specialized concepts, unusual boundaries, and excluded contents. This assessment characterizes development evidence before held-out schema validation.

## Target state

- Concept dispositions have reviewable semantic and scope reasons.
- Original assignments use the reviewed 41-concept inventory plus the residual category.
- Low-support concepts and residual profiles are inspected in context.

## Learning objectives

- Explain a human merge, rename, or exclusion.
- Interpret constrained assignments and rare concepts without an automatic frequency threshold.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 3 provides profiles and candidate concepts. The practitioner can distinguish descriptive metadata from analytical contents and explain a support denominator.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Review candidate concepts and record semantic and scope decisions. |
| **AI-ready** | Maintain structured decisions and support summaries by corpus and artifact type. |
| **AI-native** | Use constrained model assignments with residual labels and human inspection of ambiguous or unexpected mappings. |

:::info[Running example]
The study retained 41 concepts. Indicator-related merges, the visualization-type rename, and selected exclusions illustrate human decisions. Financing instruments and data-collection methods illustrate low support. The original panel_titles assignment is residual here; its later operational treatment belongs to chapter 5.
:::

## Expected artifact

A refined inventory, human decision record, and concept coverage evidence will support operational schema design.

## Implementation options

A review table and descriptive summaries are sufficient for the core workflow. Similarity or clustering can provide optional decision support for larger inventories. Such extensions are not implemented in this scaffold.

## Common mistakes

- A classification can be mistaken for a final human decision.
- Low frequency can be treated as sufficient reason for exclusion.
- Later mapping edits can be attributed retroactively to the original assessment.
