# 4. Expert Concept Refinement and Assessment

**Question:** Which concepts should remain, and how does the development evidence support them?

## Rationale

The 70 candidate concepts from [chapter 3](./concept-synthesis.mdx) are
proposals about shared meanings. Expert refinement determines whether their
distinctions serve the artifact class and intended uses. A plausible model
definition can overlap another concept, describe analytical contents, or
preserve a distinction that reviewers cannot apply consistently. Those are
semantic and scope questions that counts alone cannot settle.

Concept coverage assessment then asks how the independently discovered field
profiles relate to the reviewed inventory. It classifies each profile against
a fixed vocabulary and examines the supporting observations. This is evidence
about the development collection. It precedes operational field design and
held-out schema validation; it does not establish that a final schema is complete.

## Target state

- A reviewed concept inventory retains reasons for merges, renames, exclusions, and unresolved decisions.
- Versioned assignments connect profiles to retained concepts or an explicit residual category.
- Coverage summaries preserve their units, denominators, and evidence for specialized or ambiguous cases.

## Learning objectives

- The practitioner can document a defensible concept disposition using meaning, scope, and intended use.
- The practitioner can explain constrained classification and inspect its results without granting the model decision authority.
- The practitioner can interpret rare concepts and residual assignments without an automatic exclusion threshold.

## Prerequisite concepts

Chapter 3 supplies candidate concepts, exact-name profiles, and their underlying
observations. The reviewer understands the artifact class and its intended
descriptive uses. All worked exercises in this chapter are offline document
reviews using the prepared evidence; no API credentials are required.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | A reviewer records concept meanings, scope boundaries, and reasons for decisions. |
| **AI-ready** | Identified inventories, assignment versions, and support summaries permit repeatable inspection. |
| **AI-native** | Constrained model classification supplies traceable evidence, while humans adjudicate uncertain mappings and concept changes. |

:::info[Running example]
**Recorded study result.** Expert refinement produced 41 retained concepts from
70 candidates. The surviving inventory file also contains eight merge rows;
its 49 rows are not a complete ledger of all 70 dispositions. The subsequent
original mapping contains 833 profile assignments. **Teaching exercises.**
Selected decisions, title assignments, rare concepts, and residual cases make
those transformations inspectable without rerunning the study.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="4.1 Record concept merges, renames, and exclusions" level="Foundational" skills="Domain expert; metadata reviewer" time="45–60 minutes for the selected decisions">

**Result:** A concept-review worksheet records the proposed action, supporting evidence, semantic and scope rationale, and human disposition.

A merge asserts that one retained meaning adequately represents several
candidate meanings. A rename improves the label while preserving the intended
concept. An exclusion removes a candidate from this inventory's descriptive
scope. Review therefore needs definitions and evidence, alongside an explicit
account of which distinctions remain useful for discovery and interpretation.

**Input example.** The human-concept-decisions example in
[evidence.json](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/evidence.json) retains these definitions for three concepts considered for merging.
Compare what each meaning would preserve before reading the recorded dispositions.

| Concept under review | Recorded definition |
|---|---|
| measure_name | Name of the quantitative measure or value column reported in the snapshot. |
| axis_variable | Variable, indicator, dimension, or measure represented along a chart axis. |
| outcome_variable | Dependent variable, outcome, result, or main effect being estimated, measured, or explained. |

1. Read each candidate definition and its representative fields. Compare related concepts using descriptions and observed values. State the meaning that would be preserved or lost by a merge.
2. Assess redundancy, descriptive relevance, and distinguishability in artifact evidence. Identify an intended downstream use for a distinction that warrants retention. Preserve a borderline case for review when its scope remains uncertain.
3. Save a new working copy of the existing [concept-review template](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/concept_review_template.csv). Leave the supplied blank template unchanged. Use evidence_reference to identify the example and stage, and keep proposed_action separate from the final decision.
4. Record the target for a merge or rename and check that it exists in the reviewed inventory. Add the actual reviewer and decision reason for a new review. Mark any reconstructed explanation as a teaching interpretation.

**Worked example.** The following representative decisions are transcribed
from the paper's Table 11.

| Candidates | Recorded disposition | Recorded case-specific rationale |
|---|---|---|
| measure_name, axis_variable, outcome_variable | Merge into indicator_name. | The concepts represent equivalent analytical variables expressed through different visualization or disciplinary terminology. |
| presentation_type | Rename to visualization_type. | The name improves conceptual clarity and applicability across visualization types. |
| data_values, legend_semantics | Exclude from the reviewed inventory. | In this review, they described extracted contents outside the intended reusable metadata concepts. |

The indicator merge retains the distinction between what is measured and the
numeric observations themselves. A cost, rate, or estimated outcome can share
the retained measured-concept meaning without making its individual values
metadata. Excluding legend_semantics also does not discard every legend label:
a legend can supply a value for a retained category or indicator concept.

For a filled teaching row, use concept_name measure_name, proposed_action
merge, and target_concept indicator_name. Reference human-concept-decisions
and Table 11, state the common measured-variable meaning in semantic_rationale,
and record the reported merge in decision. A note such as “teaching transcription
of a recorded disposition; historical reviewer identity not supplied” prevents
the worksheet from masquerading as an original adjudication record.

**Verification.** A second reviewer can trace each demonstrated action to the
prepared evidence and explain its meaning or scope rationale. Merge targets
are retained concepts. The worksheet separates a current reviewer's proposed
action from the reported historical outcome. Do not infer the unrecorded
dispositions needed to reconcile every candidate from the row count alone.

**What this does not do.** The exercise does not reconstruct all 70 decisions,
their dates, or undocumented deliberations. The retained 41 rows and eight merge
rows establish an inventory and selected transformations. A complete audit history is unavailable. They do not yet determine operational fields.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="4.2 Review assignments against a fixed concept inventory" level="AI-native" skills="LLM practitioner; metadata reviewer" time="30–45 minutes for the retained assignment review">

**Result:** An assignment-review record checks selected original labels against the reviewed vocabulary, with categorical model confidence, execution status, and human review kept distinct.

Classification follows refinement because it needs stable target meanings.
Open-ended synthesis could propose new concepts; this assessment selects from
the reviewed vocabulary. Definitions and representative fields constrain the
semantic comparison. The historical residual label not_in_ontology permits a
profile to remain outside the inventory without forcing a misleading match.
It does not make the inventory a formal ontology.

The downloadable [assessment request](pathname:///cookbook-files/ai-assisted-metadata-schema-development/concept_assessment_request.md)
is an independently written, unexecuted teaching adaptation of the historical
classification task. It describes logical inputs and checks, without supplying
a live API runner.

**Worked example.** Inspect original_profile_assignments in canonical-title
and rare-and-residual, and original_profile_assignment in operational-panel-title.
The following are recorded original assignments; confidence high is the model's
judgment of fit to the fixed inventory.

| Exact-name profile | Original concept assignment | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| figure_title | title | high |
| table_title | title | high |
| chart_title | title | high |
| sample_size | not_in_ontology | high |
| panel_titles | not_in_ontology | high |

Use the request specification and the five recorded rows to review the following
classification procedure. The supplied exercise inspects existing assignments;
a new assessment would require the complete fixed reference inventory.

1. Freeze the reviewed concept names, definitions, and examples. In the study, only the 41 keep rows became concept targets. Examples from merge rows were appended to their retained targets; merged names did not become extra targets.
2. Supply one profile's metadata_field, top_description_values, and top_observed_values with that reference inventory. Compare meaning across all three attributes. Classification does not change the vocabulary while processing profiles.
3. Require one canonical_name from the retained names plus not_in_ontology, and one confidence value from high, medium, or low. Preserve execution status and the original output beside the profile identity.
4. Check allowed names and coverage of the intended input profiles. Inspect residuals, ambiguous fits, and surprising high-confidence results. Log any human correction separately and retain the original assignment for comparison.

The title rows continue chapter 3's lexical comparison. The chart_title
profile includes internal headings, so even a valid high-confidence title
assignment deserves inspection of its scope. Conversely, the residual label
for sample_size records an intentional boundary; it is not a request to add a
field automatically.

**Recorded configuration and chronology.** The classifier used GPT-5.4 mini
with medium reasoning, a Pydantic enumeration of the 41 names plus the residual,
and default API output-token and temperature settings. A later mapping changed
five assignments, including panel_titles to panel_title. The other changed
profiles were unit_of_analysis, event_context, operational_context, and
contextual_event. Those later edits are excluded from this chapter's original
coverage statistics. They do not establish that panel_title was among the 41.

**Execution-record limit.** The original mapping has 833 labelled rows. The
retained execution log contains 832 completed results and one preprocessing
failure for missing_value_marker. The mapping assigns that profile to
interpretive_note, but the inspected log supplies no successful parsed result
for it. Its resolution is undocumented. The mapping is therefore an analysis
artifact whose every row cannot be claimed as a retained successful model call.

**Verification.** Reconcile the five selected rows above against the original
assignment entries in the prepared evidence. An allowed label confirms
vocabulary compliance only. Inspect the profile's meaning, confidence, execution
status, and any review record independently. Keep mapping versions separate;
the later panel_title assignment belongs to [chapter 5](./schema-design.mdx).

**What this does not do.** No new classification is executed. Optional live
replication requires a fixed vocabulary, provider integration, a budget, and a
separate output location. Model confidence is not calibrated semantic accuracy,
and assignments do not grant authority to modify the inventory or schema.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="4.3 Examine rare concepts and residual assignments" level="AI-ready" skills="Python or spreadsheet practitioner; domain reviewer" time="30–45 minutes for the support review">

**Result:** A coverage interpretation identifies specialized concepts and residual evidence, with explicit counting units and reasons for any further review.

Frequency depends on the reporting contexts sampled. A specialized financing
concept can be important to project tables even when uncommon across the whole
collection. A residual assignment can represent analytical content, an intended
exclusion, an ambiguous fit, or a distinction that needs reconsideration.
Neither low support nor residual membership determines a schema change.

**Worked example.** The prepared rare-and-residual example reports seven
supporting snapshots for financing_instrument and nine for
data_collection_method. The following expanded counts were checked by joining
the full observations to the original assessment mapping.

| Assigned concept | Observations | Exact-name profiles | Distinct snapshots | Support out of 210 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| financing_instrument | 7 | 7 | 7 | 3.3% |
| data_collection_method | 11 | 6 | 9 | 4.3% |

1. Use the supplied summary rows and rare-and-residual observations for this review. For another collection, keep the original mapping fixed and join observations to assignments by exact metadata_field, retaining snapshot identities and source corpora. A concept can receive several differently named profiles.
2. Read observations, assigned exact-name profiles, and distinct supporting snapshots as separate counts. When computing support for a full collection, divide the distinct supporting count by its development total, which was 210 in the study. Do not add profile-level snapshot counts when their artifacts may overlap.
3. Inspect low-support concepts using their values and source contexts. Ask whether rarity reflects a specialized application, redundant meaning, or an unhelpful boundary. Inspect residual examples using the same questions.
4. Write an interpretation that separates the evidence from a proposed decision. Preserve uncertainty and the reviewed inventory unless a human approves a revision with a rationale.

These are recorded-data summaries for the full development collection. They
cannot be recomputed from the few observations shipped in the evidence package.
For data_collection_method, counting 11 observations as 11 snapshots would
overstate support. For financing_instrument, equal counts are a property of
these records. Other concepts need their counts checked separately.

The Pakistan project table's recorded value “IDA Credit; IDA Grant; Guarantee
Amount” explains why financing modality is meaningful. All seven supporting
snapshots occur in the Refugee Project Appraisal Document corpus. That
concentration supports a specialized interpretation; it does not establish
universal usefulness or a minimum inclusion threshold.

The Lebanon health-access figure separately proposed sample_size with value
“n=44.” Its exact-name profile has 15 observations from 15 snapshots, and the
original classification is residual. The figure's recorded title also includes
“(n=44).” Preserving an explicit title or explanatory statement in a later
operational record is a different question from accepting a dedicated sample-size
concept. [Chapter 5](./schema-design.mdx) demonstrates that distinction without
rewriting the coverage assignment.

**Verification.** Calculate 7 / 210 and 9 / 210 and round to one decimal
percentage point. Explain why six method profiles can contribute 11 observations
but only nine distinct snapshots. A defensible interpretation identifies the
source context and scope rationale before recommending retention or revision.

**What this does not do.** The summaries characterize the selected development
evidence. They do not measure retrieval performance, establish a missing-field
rate, prove generalizability, or replace held-out validation. The study found no
compelling basis for further conceptual revision before operational design.

</Recipe>

## Expected artifact

A reviewed inventory of 41 concepts, representative human decisions, and
versioned coverage evidence supply the basis for operational design. The
inventory describes meaningful distinctions; [chapter 5](./schema-design.mdx)
defines the fields practitioners will use to describe artifacts consistently.

## Implementation options

A spreadsheet and the existing concept-review template are sufficient for the
selected exercises. Larger collections can use joins and distinct counts with
explicit inventory and mapping versions. Model classification can reduce manual
comparison effort while leaving review authority with humans. Similarity tools
or clustering would be proposed extensions; they are unnecessary for this review.

## Common mistakes

- A 49-row inventory can be mistaken for a complete ledger of 70 candidate dispositions.
- A valid concept label or high confidence can be mistaken for a final human judgment.
- Later mapping revisions can silently change the interpretation of the original assessment.
- Observation totals or summed profile counts can be mistaken for distinct concept-supporting snapshots.
- Low frequency or residual membership can be used as an automatic field-design rule.

## Verification

- Every demonstrated merge, rename, and exclusion has an attributable rationale and the correct stage.
- Selected original assignments use the reviewed inventory plus the residual, without the later panel_title target.
- Support interpretations preserve the 210-snapshot denominator and the difference between profiles, observations, and artifacts.
- The original mapping and its execution-record limitation remain distinguishable from subsequent revisions.
