# 4. Dataset cards

**Question:** How is a dataset documented for the people who train models on it?

## Rationale

A person who trains a model on a dataset needs to know things the
dictionary and the loading record do not say. Why does the dataset
exist? How were the examples collected and labelled, and how reliable
are the labels? What was removed or changed? Whom do the examples
represent, and whom do they leave out? What is the dataset for, what
must it never be used for, under which terms, and how is it cited? A dataset card is the one
document that answers these questions, in a fixed order, on the
dataset's page. The idea comes from the datasheets proposal of Gebru
and others (2018) and from the data cards of Pushkarna and others
(2022); dataset repositories made the card the first thing a user sees.

For a statistical organization the card has two extra duties. It
states that the dataset is a collection of examples and not a source
of statistics, so that nobody computes a rate from it. And it carries
the representativeness report of [chapter 5](./represent.mdx), so that
a model builder knows which groups the examples cover thinly. A card
is written by the people who made the dataset and checked for
completeness by a script, so that no release goes out with a section
missing.

## Target state

- Every dataset has a card with the twelve sections of this chapter, written from the decision record, the collection notes, and the measurements.
- The card opens with a machine-readable header (licence, language, task, size) that repositories and catalogs read.
- The card is checked for completeness on every release, and reviewed by a person for accuracy.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write the card from the template for one dataset; run the check. |
| **AI-ready** | Write the representativeness and known-gaps section from the report of chapter 5; publish the card on the dataset page and in the repository; review it with a subject-matter specialist. |
| **AI-native** | Generate the measured sections of the card at each release from the scripts, and keep the written sections under version control with the data. |

:::info[Running example]
The card of the occupation-coding dataset says where the job
descriptions come from, how they were coded and with what agreement
between coders, what was removed, how the records differ from the
population, what the dataset is for, what it is not for, its licence,
its version, and how to cite it.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="4.1 Write the card from the template" level="Foundational" skills="Dataset owner; methodologist" time="Half a day per dataset">

**Result:** the dataset has a card with a machine-readable header and
twelve sections, which is its documentation for users.

The card is a Markdown file. It opens with a YAML header (a short
block of key and value pairs between two lines of three dashes) that
repositories read for filtering: the licence identifier, the
languages, the task, the size, and tags. The twelve sections follow.
The card of the running example:

```md file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/ml-ready-datasets/dataset_card.md
```

The sections and what each answers:

| Section | What it answers |
|---|---|
| Dataset summary | What the dataset is, in one paragraph, including the task it supports |
| Motivation | Why it was made and for whom |
| Composition | How many records, which columns, which splits |
| Collection process | Where the examples come from, how the labels were assigned, and how reliable they are |
| Preprocessing | What was removed, changed, or anonymized |
| Representativeness and known gaps | Whom the examples represent, compared with the population, and which groups are thin ([chapter 5](./represent.mdx)) |
| Intended uses | The uses the dataset was made for |
| Out-of-scope uses | The uses it must not be put to, including estimation and re-identification |
| Licence and terms | The licence, the attribution form, and the terms for training ([chapter 7](./license.mdx)) |
| Versions | The current version, what changed, and what is frozen ([chapter 8](./version.mdx)) |
| Citation | The citation text with a DOI or URL |
| Contact | Whom to ask |

1. Fill the header: the licence identifier (`cc-by-4.0`), the
   languages of the text fields (ISO 639-1 codes), the task
   (`text-classification` for coding), the size category, and tags.
2. Write the summary, motivation, and intended and out-of-scope uses
   from the decision record of [chapter 1](./purpose.mdx).
3. Write the collection process with the facts a model builder needs:
   who wrote the inputs, who assigned the labels, with which
   instrument, and how well two labellers agree. The agreement between
   coders is the ceiling of what a model can reach.
4. Write the preprocessing section as a list of what was done to the
   source records and nothing else. "Spelling was not corrected" is as
   useful as what was.
5. Leave the representativeness section for [chapter 5](./represent.mdx)
   and the licence section for [chapter 7](./license.mdx), and fill
   them when those chapters are done.

**Worked example.** The collection process section of the running
example's card reads, in part: "Job descriptions were written by
interviewers from respondents' answers to the question on the kind of
work done in the main job in the reference week. Codes were assigned by six trained coders using the ISCO-08 index, with
a second coder on a tenth of the records. The two coders agreed on 91
percent of four-digit codes and 97 percent of major groups." The
preprocessing section lists four things. Employer names were replaced
by a generic term. Place names below the region were removed. Spelling
was not corrected. Records coded 9999 were kept. The out-of-scope section opens with "Do
not estimate the share of any occupation or group from this dataset;
it is a collection of examples and is not a sample of the population."

**What this does not do.** A card documents. It does not make the
collection process sound or the labels reliable, and a card that
overstates either misleads every user. The subject-matter review of
recipe 4.3 is where accuracy is checked.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="4.2 Check the card before release" level="Foundational" skills="Python" time="Minutes">

**Result:** a check runs on every release and confirms that the card
has its header fields and its twelve sections. It also checks four
statements: that the out-of-scope section lists a use, that the
representativeness section states a number, that the citation has a DOI
or a URL, and that the versions section names a version.

The script reads the card and reports each check:

```python file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/ml-ready-datasets/check_dataset_card.py
```

```bash
python check_dataset_card.py dataset_card.md
```

Output for the running example:

```text
  ok       front matter: license
  ok       front matter: language
  ok       front matter: task_categories
  ok       front matter: pretty_name
  ok       Dataset summary
  ok       Motivation
  ok       Composition
  ok       Collection process
  ok       Preprocessing
  ok       Representativeness and known gaps
  ok       Intended uses
  ok       Out-of-scope uses
  ok       Licence and terms
  ok       Versions
  ok       Citation
  ok       Contact
  ok       out-of-scope section lists a use
  ok       representativeness section states a number
  ok       citation has a DOI or URL
  ok       versions section names a version
result: PASS
```

The first four lines confirm the header fields, the next twelve confirm
the sections, and the last four confirm the statements. The verdict
follows.

1. Run the check on the card. A missing section or statement blocks
   the release.
2. Add the organization's own rules to the script: a sentence that
   must appear ("not a source of published statistics"), a maximum
   age of the benchmark used in the representativeness section, the
   contact's domain.
3. Keep the check in the release pipeline with the Croissant check and
   the manifest.

**Worked example.** The first draft of the card failed the check on two
lines: the representativeness section was a placeholder with no number,
because chapter 5 had not yet run, and the citation had no URL. After
the representativeness report was written in and the citation gained
the dataset's URL, the check passed. The data manager then added one
organization-specific rule to the script, which requires the sentence
"not a source of published statistics" somewhere in the card, and the
check now fails any card that omits it.

**What this does not do.** The check reads presence. A section that is
present and wrong passes. Recipe 4.3 is the review for that.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="4.3 Review the card and keep it current" level="AI-ready" skills="Subject-matter specialist; dataset owner" time="One hour per release">

**Result:** a specialist has confirmed the card's statements against the
source, and the card is updated with every version of the dataset.

1. Have a specialist who knows the source survey read the collection
   process, preprocessing, and representativeness sections against the
   survey's own documentation. Record the review and the date in the
   versions section.
2. Regenerate the measured sections (composition, representativeness)
   from the scripts at each release, so that the numbers in the card
   are the numbers of the files beside it.
3. When a user reports something the card did not say (a group the
   model fails on, a labelling rule they did not know), add it to the
   known gaps or the collection process. Release the corrected card as
   a new patch version with the same data.
4. Keep the card under version control with the data, and publish it
   on the dataset page, in the repository, and as the description of
   the catalog record.

**Worked example.** The labour statistician who runs the survey reads
the card against the survey's documentation and corrects two statements. The coders used index version 4, where the
card said version 3, and the second-coder sample was an eighth of the
records, where the card said a tenth. The
review is recorded in the versions section with her initials and the
date. Three months after release, a partner reports that the model it
trained fails on job descriptions in French, which the card had not
mentioned. The known gaps section gains the sentence "about 12 percent
of job descriptions are in French, concentrated in the Northern
Region", and the card is released as version 1.0.1 with the same data.

**What this does not do.** A card can only say what its authors know.
The representativeness report measures part of the rest, and users'
findings supply the remainder over time, which is why the card has a
contact and a version.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Template:** the twelve sections of this chapter; the Hugging Face dataset card template, which has similar sections and the same header; a datasheet with its question list.
- **Header:** the YAML keys that repositories read (`license`, `language`, `task_categories`, `size_categories`, `pretty_name`, `tags`).
- **Where it lives:** the release folder as `dataset_card.md`; the dataset page; the catalog record's description; the repository's README.

- [Datasheets for Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010) (Gebru and others, 2018): the questions on motivation, composition, collection, preprocessing, uses, distribution, and maintenance that a card answers.
- [Data Cards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01075) (Pushkarna and others, 2022): structured summaries of a dataset's essential facts for decisions across its lifecycle.
- [Metadata Editor](https://github.com/worldbank/metadata-editor): where the catalog record that the card's description feeds is kept.

## Common mistakes

- **A card written once.** Numbers in the card drift from the files; the measured sections are regenerated.
- **Label reliability unstated.** A model builder cannot tell a model's error from the coders' error.
- **No out-of-scope section.** Someone computes an employment rate from the examples.

## Verification

- **Completeness.** The check passes on every release.
- **Accuracy.** A specialist's review is recorded in the versions section.
- **Currency.** The composition and representativeness numbers match the current files.
