# 1. Purpose and selection of datasets

**Question:** Which datasets should be published for machine learning, and for what uses?

## Rationale

A machine-learning model learns from examples. A model that assigns occupation codes learns from job descriptions
that a coder has already coded. A model that reads scanned tables learns
from pages that a person has already transcribed. A model that answers
questions about statistics is evaluated on questions whose answers are
known. A
statistical organization produces such examples every day as a
by-product of its work, and most of them never leave the building.
Published in the right form, they let the organization train its own
models, let partners build tools that work on the organization's data,
and let researchers evaluate methods on real official statistics.

An ML-ready dataset is a dataset packaged so that it can be loaded,
understood, and used for training or evaluation without a
conversation with its producer. It differs from an AI-ready catalog in
what it is for. The catalog (the
[dissemination cookbook](/cookbook/ai-ready-dissemination/)) makes
published statistics findable and retrievable by AI systems at the
moment a question is asked. An ML-ready dataset is a fixed, versioned
set of examples with a documented structure, a stated purpose, splits
for training and testing, and a licence that covers model training.
The program's AI-ready data attributes name three properties for
training data beyond the foundational ones: integrative (linkable
across datasets), quantity (enough for the task), and diversity and
representativeness (the examples cover the population they will be
used on). This guide is about publishing datasets that have those
properties and say so.

## Target state

- The organization has a written statement of what it publishes as ML-ready datasets, for which uses, and under which conditions.
- Candidate datasets are identified from the organization's own tasks and from demand, and the decision to publish each is recorded with its intended uses.
- Every published dataset has a named owner and a place in the catalog beside the organization's other products.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Write the statement; publish one dataset from an existing coding or transcription task, with its card and record. |
| **AI-ready** | Publish datasets for the main model-assisted tasks, each selected by a written rule, with splits, a Croissant record, a representativeness report, and a manifest. |
| **AI-native** | Produce ML-ready datasets as a stage of production: each round of a survey adds coded examples to a versioned dataset with its documentation regenerated. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization codes the occupations of its labour force
survey by hand. It wants a model that proposes codes, and it wants
partners who use ISCO-08 to be able to build and compare such models.
It publishes 600 coded job descriptions with the respondent's region,
sex, age group, education, and weight, as a worked example of the
format. All records are invented. The chapters of this guide select,
shape, describe, document, split, license, and version that dataset.
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="1.1 Write the statement of purpose" level="Foundational" skills="Head of dissemination; methodologist" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the organization has a short, approved statement of what it
publishes as ML-ready datasets, what they may be used for, and what
they may not be used for.

The statement sets expectations for users and for staff. It is
published with the organization's data policy and repeated, in short,
in every dataset card.

1. State the purpose: datasets for training and evaluating models on
   the organization's own tasks (coding, extraction, search, question
   answering), published so that the organization, its partners, and
   researchers work on the same examples.
2. State the conditions. At minimum:
   - no record that could identify a person or an enterprise is
     published; datasets are built from public use data, from
     aggregates, or from synthetic data
     ([synthetic data cookbook](/cookbook/synthetic-data-for-sharing/));
   - every dataset has a card, a loading record, a representativeness
     report, a licence, and a version;
   - no dataset is a source of published statistics. A dataset is a
     collection of examples, and its composition is stated in the card.
3. Name who approves a publication: the owner of the source data and
   the disclosure control unit.
4. Publish the statement and link it from every dataset page.

**Worked example.** The example organization's statement opens with its purpose. The
office publishes datasets of examples from its own work, such as coded
survey responses and transcribed tables, so that models for its tasks
can be trained and compared on real official statistics. Its conditions
follow. Every dataset is built from a public use file, from aggregates,
or from synthetic data. Every dataset is released with a card, a
loading record, a representativeness report, a licence, and a version.
No dataset is a source of published statistics. The owner of the source data and the head of the
disclosure control unit approve each publication. The statement is
published as an annex to the data policy, and every dataset card
repeats its three conditions in its out-of-scope section.

**What this does not do.** A statement sets the rules. Whether a
particular dataset meets them is decided dataset by dataset in the
later chapters, and recorded in its card.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.2 List the candidate datasets from the organization's tasks" level="Foundational" skills="Methodologist; production managers" time="One day">

**Result:** the organization has a list of the datasets it could
publish, each with the task it serves, the source it comes from, the
number of examples, and the disclosure question it raises.

Most candidates come from work the organization already does by hand.
Each such task produces pairs of an input and a correct output, which
is what a model learns from:

| Task the organization does | Examples it produces | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Occupation and industry coding | Job description with its code | Labour force survey, census |
| Editing and imputation | Record before and after an editor's decision | Processing systems |
| Transcription of printed tables | Page image with its transcribed table | Document archive ([documents cookbook](/cookbook/data-from-documents/)) |
| Search for a series | Question with the series it refers to | Search logs, help desk ([evaluation cookbook](/cookbook/evaluation-suites/)) |
| Metadata curation | Record with the curator's corrections | Catalog ([curation cookbook](/cookbook/metadata-curation-with-llms/)) |
| Translation of labels | Label with its reviewed translation | Multilingual catalog |

1. Walk through the production process with the people who run it
   and list every task of this kind, with the system that holds the
   examples.
2. For each, count the examples and note the disclosure question. A
   job description is free text written by a respondent and can
   identify a person; a question from a search log can contain a name.
3. Order the list by the value of a model for the task (the
   [production cookbook](/cookbook/language-models-in-production/map)
   maps the tasks) and by demand from partners.
4. Choose one to start with. A coding task with a few thousand coded
   examples and a clear disclosure treatment is the usual first choice.

**Worked example.** The walk through production yields six candidates.
Occupation coding has about 38,000 coded job descriptions over four
rounds, with the disclosure question that a description can name an
employer. Industry coding has the same records. Editing has 12,000
before-and-after pairs, with the question that the full record is
confidential. Table transcription has 1,900 verified tables from the
documents cookbook, with no disclosure question at all, because the
pages are published. Catalog search has 410 questions with their
series. Translation has 2,300 reviewed label pairs. The list is ordered
with occupation coding first, because a coding model is wanted and
partners have asked for it, and transcription second. The 600-record
extract in this guide stands in for the first.

**What this does not do.** The list says what could be published. The
disclosure control unit decides what can be, dataset by dataset
([chapter 7](./license.mdx)), and the representativeness report of
[chapter 5](./represent.mdx) says what the examples cover.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="1.3 Record the decision to publish" level="AI-ready" skills="Dataset owner" time="One hour per dataset">

**Result:** each dataset the organization publishes has a record of
why it is published, for which uses, from which source, under whose
ownership, and with which approvals. The record becomes the first part
of the dataset's card.

1. Write the intended uses in the users' terms: training a coder for
   surveys that use ISCO-08; benchmarking coding models; teaching.
2. Write the out-of-scope uses: estimating population figures from the
   examples; identifying respondents; any use the licence excludes.
3. Name the owner (a unit and a contact), the source data and its
   round, and the approvals (data owner, disclosure control).
4. File the record with the dataset's documentation. The card of
   [chapter 4](./document.mdx) carries the same content for users.

**Worked example.** The decision record for the occupation-coding
dataset lists as intended uses: training an automatic coder for surveys
that use ISCO-08, comparing coding models on a common test part, and
teaching occupation coding. Its out-of-scope uses are estimating
employment or occupation shares from the examples, identifying
respondents or employers, and any use the licence excludes. The owner
is the labour statistics unit, with the coding supervisor as contact.
The source is the public use file of the 2025 Q2 labour force survey,
with the job descriptions screened as chapter 7 describes. The
approvals are the head of the labour statistics unit and the head of
the disclosure control unit, with the dates. The record is filed in the
dataset's folder.

**What this does not do.** A decision record is not a release. The
release happens when the files, the card, the loading record, the
licence, and the manifest of the later chapters exist and have been
checked.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Scope of the statement:** training and evaluation data for the organization's own tasks; benchmark datasets for the community; both.
- **Sources of examples:** by-products of production (coding, editing, transcription), curated collections (question sets, labelled samples), synthetic data where the real records cannot be published.
- **Where datasets live:** the organization's catalog as a data type of its own; a public repository for machine-learning datasets in addition, with the catalog record as the authority.

## World Bank examples

- The program's [Data Snapshots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06242) dataset publishes annotated pages from institutional documents for layout detection, with the annotation format and licence the [documents cookbook](/cookbook/data-from-documents/locate) describes.
- The [PI-FT toolkit](/pift-toolkit/pipeline) fine-tunes retrieval models on structured records and reports evaluation results, which is the use an ML-ready catalog extract serves.
- The program's classification and coding workstream develops automatic occupation and industry coding, the task of the running example.

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing an extract of microdata as "training data".** An ML-ready dataset has a task, a label, and a card; an extract without them is microdata under another name, with microdata's disclosure risk.
- **Starting with the largest dataset.** Start with the task where a model is wanted and the examples are clean.
- **No owner.** A dataset without an owner is never updated and never withdrawn.

## Verification

- **Statement.** The statement is approved and linked from every dataset page.
- **Candidate list.** The list exists with counts and disclosure notes, ordered by value and demand.
- **Decision record.** Each published dataset has a decision record with its uses, source, owner, and approvals.
