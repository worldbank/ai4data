# 3. Risk rating of components

**Question:** How much could each component cost the organization if it fails?

## Rationale

Not every AI component deserves the same controls. A model that drafts a
description a curator reads before publication can do limited harm. A
model that drafts the commentary of a release, or an assistant that quotes
figures to the public under the organization's name, can put a wrong number
in front of the press or a ministry. Treating the two alike wastes effort
on the first and under-protects the second.

A risk rating makes the difference explicit and proportionate. Three
ratings, each from 1 to 3, describe what an error would cost, how many
people would see it, and how hard it would be to undo. Their product places
the component in a tier, and each tier carries a fixed list of controls.
The rating is a judgment, made by people who know the component; the
script makes the arithmetic and the consequences visible and keeps the
register's tier in step with the rating.

## Target state

- Every component in the register has a rating on cost of error, exposure, and reversibility, with its worst case written down.
- The tier in the register equals the tier the rating gives.
- The controls the tier requires are in place, and each has evidence.
- The residual risk of each component is accepted by the role the tier names, on a date.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Rate each component, write its worst case, and record the tier in the register. |
| **AI-ready** | Put the controls of each tier in place with their evidence; record the acceptance of residual risk; re-rate on each change of model, data, or audience. |
| **AI-native** | Feed the suite scores and the incident record back into the ratings; report the tiers and their controls to the board each quarter. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization's risk register rates its six components. Three
score 18 and are High tier: occupation coding, release commentary drafts,
and the public assistant. Edit failure explanations score 8 and are
Medium. The two dissemination components score 2 and are Low. The files

## Recipes

<Recipe title="3.1 Rate each component on cost of error, exposure, and reversibility" level="Foundational" skills="The component's owner with the head of methodology" time="Half an hour per component">

**Result:** each component has three ratings, a score, a tier, and a
written worst case, and the register's tier matches.

The three ratings describe different things, and the product of the three
is the score because a high rating on any one of them multiplies the
others. Cost of error asks what the worst plausible error would cost: a
wrong word in a description, or a wrong figure in a published statistic.
Exposure asks how many people see the output before a person checks it:
one curator, a unit, or the public. Reversibility asks how hard the error
is to undo once it is out: a corrected record, a correction notice, or a
figure quoted in a parliamentary debate.

| Rating | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost of error | Inconvenience inside the organization | A wrong published element that users rely on | A wrong official figure, or harm to a respondent |
| Exposure | One reviewer sees the output first | A unit or a sample of users sees it | The public or the press sees it |
| Reversibility | Undone by editing a record | Undone with a correction notice | Not fully undone once quoted |

The score runs from 1 to 27. A score up to 6 is Low, 7 to 12 Medium, and
13 or more High.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `cost_of_error`, `exposure`, `reversibility` | The three ratings, 1 to 3 |
| `worst_case` | The error the ratings describe, in one sentence |
| `controls_in_place` | The controls that hold today |
| `residual_risk_accepted_by`, `accepted_on` | The role that accepted what remains, and when |

```csv file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/governance-and-risk/risk_register.csv
```

1. For each component, write the worst case first. Ratings made without
   a worst case in front of the raters drift toward the middle.
2. Rate the three dimensions against the table. Where the owner and the
   head of methodology differ, the higher rating stands until the board
   decides.
3. Run the script with the register. It computes the scores and tiers,
   compares them with the register, and lists the controls each tier
   requires.

```bash
python risk_score.py risk_register.csv controls_by_tier.csv --register component_register.csv
```

```text
id   component                      cost  exp  rev score  tier
C05  Release commentary drafts         3    3    2    18  High
C03  Occupation coding                 3    2    3    18  High
C06  Public statistics assistant       3    3    2    18  High
C04  Edit failure explanations         2    2    2     8  Medium
C02  Catalog semantic search           1    2    1     2  Low
C01  Metadata description drafts       2    1    1     2  Low

controls required:
  Low (C01, C02):
    - A row in the register with an owner and an exit plan [Register row]
    - Human review of outputs that reach a published product [Review record]
  Medium (C04):
    - All Low controls [Register row; review record]
    - An evaluation suite with a required score before each change [Suite report]
    - A statement of model use with each release that used the component [Statement of model use]
  High (C03, C05, C06):
    - All Medium controls [As above]
    - Residual risk accepted by the Director General in the decision log [Decision log entry]
    - A switch-off test on record and an incident procedure [Decision log entry; incident report]
    - A sample of outputs reviewed on a schedule and reported to the board [Review report]
result: PASS
```

**Worked example.** The commentary drafts (C05) rate 3 on cost of error, because a wrong figure in a release commentary is a wrong official statement. They rate 3 on exposure, because the commentary goes to the press, and 2 on reversibility, because a correction notice can be issued. The score is
18 and the tier High. Occupation coding (C03) rates 3 on reversibility,
because a systematic miscoding changes a published distribution for a
quarter that cannot be recalled, and 2 on exposure, because the coders see
the routed share first. The metadata drafts (C01) rate 2, 1, 1: a wrong
definition misleads users of one series, one curator sees every draft, and
the record is edited. The script's tiers match the register for all six
rows.

**What this does not do.** The ratings are the judgment of the people who
rate; the script does not make them. A rating is reread when the
component's audience, data, or model changes, and when an incident shows
that a worst case was underrated.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="3.2 Apply the controls the tier requires" level="AI-ready" skills="The component's owner; the owners of the suites and the statement" time="From one hour for a Low component to several days for a High one">

**Result:** every component has the controls its tier requires, and for
each control there is a piece of evidence that it is in place.

The controls table ties each tier to a short list, and each control to the
evidence that shows it. Low components need a register row and a review
of what reaches a product. Medium components add an evaluation suite with
a required score and a statement of model use with each release. High
components add the acceptance of residual risk at the top, a switch-off
test and an incident procedure, and a sample of outputs reviewed on a
schedule and reported to the board.

```csv file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/governance-and-risk/controls_by_tier.csv
```

1. For each component, take the controls of its tier from the script's
   output.
2. For each control, name the evidence and where it lives: the register
   row, the suite report, the statement, the decision log entry, the
   review report.
3. Put the evidence names in the `controls_in_place` column of the risk
   register, so that the governance review of chapter 8 can find them.
4. A control that cannot be evidenced is not in place. The component
   waits, or the board accepts the gap explicitly and gives it a date.

**Worked example.** The commentary drafts (C05) are High tier. Their
register row and the analyst's approval record cover the Low controls.
The commentary suite, with its required score and the reference-period
case added after the incident of chapter 4, covers the suite control, and
the statement of model use of the labour market report covers the
statement control. The Director General accepted the residual risk in
decision D006. The switch-off test is the exit plan "analysts draft from
the tables", tried when the second provider was tested, and the incident
procedure is the one that handled I-2025-01. The monthly sample is the
analyst's number check on every draft, reported to the board each
quarter.

**What this does not do.** The controls list says what the tier needs;
the suites and the statement are described in the evaluation and
production cookbooks. A control's evidence is only as good as the
measurement behind it, and a suite with a low required score is a weak
control with strong paperwork.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="3.3 Record the acceptance of residual risk" level="AI-ready" skills="The role the tier names; the board's secretary records" time="Part of the approval decision">

**Result:** for every component, a named role has accepted what remains
after the controls, on a date, and the acceptance is in the risk register
and the decision log.

Controls reduce risk; they do not remove it. A coding model with a
threshold and a re-coded sample still miscodes some responses. An
assistant with every number verified can still misread a question. The
organization decides to run the component anyway, and governance means
that the decision is taken by someone with the authority to take it and
is written down. The tier says who: the head of methodology for Low and
Medium, the Director General for High.

1. At approval (recipe 2.2), the accepting role reads the worst case, the
   controls, and the suite report.
2. The role accepts or declines. An acceptance is recorded in the risk
   register's `residual_risk_accepted_by` and `accepted_on` columns and in
   the approval decision's text.
3. A change that moves a component to a higher tier needs a new
   acceptance by the new role.

**Worked example.** The three High components were accepted by the
Director General: occupation coding on 2025-10-06 (decision D004), the
commentary drafts on 2025-11-03 (D006), and the public assistant on
2025-12-01 (D007). The decision text of each says "residual risk
accepted". The Medium and Low components were accepted by the head of
methodology on the dates of their approval. The decision log check of
chapter 4 verifies that every High-tier approval was decided by the
Director General.

**What this does not do.** Acceptance is a decision about the known
worst case. An incident that reveals a worse case than the one written
down reopens the rating, the controls, and the acceptance; chapter 4
describes how.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Scales.** Three ratings on a 1 to 3 scale are enough for a register of a few dozen components. Larger organizations with a risk function can map the ratings onto its scale, as long as the tiers and their controls stay the same.
- **Frameworks.** The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [ISO/IEC 23894](https://www.iso.org/standard/77304.html) describe AI risk management in general terms. The tiers of this chapter are a proportionate implementation for a statistical organization.
- **Quality frameworks.** The risk register sits beside the quality management of the [UN NQAF manual](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/dataquality/) and the [European Statistics Code of Practice](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/quality/european-statistics-code-of-practice): a wrong official figure is a quality failure, and the rating names it as such.

## World Bank examples

- The [Evaluation Suites](/cookbook/evaluation-suites/inventory) cookbook orders the inventory of components by cost of error, which is the first of the three ratings here.
- The [Proof-Carrying Numbers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06902) method is the control that keeps the public assistant's exposure rating from being paired with a reversibility of 3: a wrong number is flagged before it is shown.

## Common mistakes

- **Ratings without a worst case.** Raters who have not written the error down rate everything 2.
- **A tier in the register that drifted from the rating.** The script compares the two; the register is what people read.
- **Controls without evidence.** A control that exists in a sentence is found missing at the governance review.
- **Acceptance by the wrong role.** A High-tier component accepted by its own owner has not been accepted.

## Verification

- **Score check.** The script passes with the register: every tier matches, every residual risk is accepted.
- **Evidence check.** For a High-tier component picked at random, each control's evidence can be opened.
- **Re-rating check.** A component whose model, data, or audience changed since its rating has a new rating with a date after the change.
