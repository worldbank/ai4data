# 8. The governance review before a re-assessment

**Question:** Is the governance ready for a re-assessment?

## Rationale

The AI-readiness assessment framework scores an organization's strategy
and governance, its legal environment, and its data licensing among its
twelve dimensions. The questions it asks under those dimensions are
answered by artefacts: a policy, a register, a risk register, a decision
log, an incident procedure, a provider register, a licence matrix, a
statement of model use. An organization that has followed this guide has
them. The review is where it checks that they are in order and assembles
them as evidence, so that the assessment can read documents where it would otherwise hear accounts.

The review is also the organization's own look at a year of governance. A
check that fails, a follow-up that is overdue, a licence that is missing,
are findings whether or not an assessment is coming, and the actions they
produce are the governance work of the next cycle.

## Target state

- Every governance check runs in one pass, and each artefact's result is known.
- Each artefact is mapped to the assessment questions it is evidence for, and the questions this guide does not cover are known.
- The evidence pack exists as a list of artefacts with their locations and dates.
- The findings of the review are actions in the decision log with owners and due dates.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Run the review once by hand: open each artefact and note whether it exists and is current. |
| **AI-ready** | Run the review script before each board meeting and before a re-assessment; compile the evidence pack; record the actions. |
| **AI-native** | Run the review in continuous integration on every change to the governance files, and publish the review's summary with the statement of model use. |

:::info[Running example]
The example organization ran its governance review on 2026-01-15 against
the statement of the second-quarter labour market report. Ten of eleven
items were in order; the licence matrix failed on two products. The script
is
[governance_review.py](pathname:///cookbook-files/governance-and-risk/governance_review.py).
:::

## Recipes

<Recipe title="8.1 Run the governance review" level="AI-ready" skills="Head of methodology; the board reads the result" time="Half a day, most of it reading">

**Result:** one table that says, for each governance artefact, whether its
check passed or the document is present, and which assessment questions
it is evidence for.

The review script runs the checks of chapters 1 to 7 on the files in a
folder and lists the documents that have no check. Each line names the
assessment questions the artefact answers, using the framework's
numbering: 1.1 for strategy and governance, 1.2 for the legal
environment, 1.5 for funding and resources, and 2.6 for data licensing.
The script reads nothing for meaning; a document that exists and a check
that passes are what it reports, and the people in the review read the
rest.

1. Put the current versions of the artefacts in one folder, with the file
   names of this guide.
2. Run the review with the date and the latest release.

```bash
python governance_review.py . --as-of 2026-01-15 --release EX-REP-2025-02
```

```text
governance review as of 2026-01-15
artefact                           result   evidence for questions
AI-use policy                      PASS     1.1.1, 1.1.4, 1.2.7
Component register                 PASS     1.1.2, 1.1.3, 1.2.7
Risk register                      PASS     1.1.3
Decision log                       PASS     1.1.2, 1.2.7
Licence matrix and model licences  FAIL     1.2.5, 2.6.1, 2.6.2, 2.6.3, 2.6.4
Statement of model use             PASS     1.1.4, 1.2.7
Board terms of reference           present  1.1.2
Incident report                    present  1.1.3, 1.2.7
Provider register                  present  1.2.5, 1.5.4
Cost record                        present  1.5.4
Terms of use for machine clients   present  2.6.4

10 of 11 items in order
result: FAIL
```

3. For each FAIL, run the artefact's own check to read the findings.
4. Read the artefacts that are only "present" for currency: the terms of
   reference against the board's actual membership, the incident report
   against the log, the provider register's test dates, the cost record's
   last month.

**Worked example.** On 2026-01-15 the example organization's review found
ten of eleven items in order. The licence check failed on two products:
the food security report's licence was on the page and not in its record,
and the statistical yearbook had no licence. The reading of the "present"
documents found the provider register's drills within the year, the cost
record complete to December, and the board's membership as the terms
state. The decision log check, which passed, listed two overdue follow-ups
on High-tier components; the review took them as findings although the
check did not fail on them.

**What this does not do.** The review checks the artefacts this guide
maintains. The assessment also asks about an AI strategy, change
management, the legal framework for AI, and access to public and private
data, which are decisions and documents of the organization and its
government; recipe 8.2 names them so that the gap is known.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="8.2 Compile the evidence pack for the assessment" level="AI-ready" skills="Head of methodology; the assessment's coordinator" time="One day">

**Result:** a list, by assessment question, of the artefact that answers
it, where it is, and its date, and a list of the questions this guide does
not answer.

The evidence pack turns the review's table into the form the assessment
uses: one line per question. Most questions of the governance and legal
dimensions and all four of the licensing dimension have an artefact in
this guide. The remainder are named so that the organization knows what
it has to produce elsewhere.

| Assessment question | Evidence from this guide | Outside this guide |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1.1 AI strategy | The policy's scope and principles; the task boundary | A strategy document that places AI in the organization's plan |
| 1.1.2 Governance and oversight | The board's terms of reference; the decision log | |
| 1.1.3 AI risk management | The risk register with tiers and controls; the incident report | |
| 1.1.4 Ethical guidelines and principles | The policy's principles and prohibited uses; the statement of model use | |
| 1.1.5 Change management | | The organization's change and training plan; see the [AI assistants](/cookbook/ai-assistants-at-work/unit) cookbook for a unit's adoption |
| 1.2.1 to 1.2.4 Legal frameworks and data access | | The statistics act and the data protection rules, reviewed by the legal adviser |
| 1.2.5 Third-party data and intellectual property | The provider register's data terms; the model licence table | |
| 1.2.6 Open-source publication of code | | The organization's repository and its licence |
| 1.2.7 Accountability for AI-generated outputs | The policy's roles; the decision log; the statement of model use; the incident report | |
| 1.5.4 Tracking value and return | The cost record and the review outcomes | The organization's budget lines |
| 2.6.1 to 2.6.4 Data licensing | The licence matrix; the terms for machine clients | |

1. For each question with evidence, write the artefact's location (the
   repository path or the published URL) and the date of its last check.
2. For each question without evidence, name the owner of the document
   that would answer it and the date it is expected.
3. Attach the review's table and the artefacts' own check outputs.

**Worked example.** The example organization's pack lists ten artefacts with their dates. The policy was published on 2025-09-15 and checked on 2026-01-15. The register, the risk register, and the decision log were checked on the same day, and the board's terms of reference date from 2025-09-15. The incident report I-2025-01 was closed on 2025-12-05. The provider register records drills on 2025-12-10 and 2025-12-18, and the cost record runs to December 2025. The licence matrix was checked on 2026-01-15 with two findings and their actions. The terms for machine clients are version 1.0 of 2025-12-01, and the statement of model use of the second-quarter labour market report was signed on release. Outside
the guide it names the AI strategy document, owned by the Director
General's office, and the legal review of the statistics act, owned by
the legal adviser, both expected before the re-assessment.

**What this does not do.** The pack is evidence; the assessment's scoring
is the assessor's reading of it against the framework's maturity levels.
The [assessment to pathway](/cookbook/assessment-to-pathway/evidence)
cookbook describes the evidence register across all twelve dimensions.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="8.3 Record the actions for the next cycle" level="Foundational" skills="The board's secretary" time="One hour">

**Result:** every finding of the review is an action in the decision log
with an owner and a due date, and the next review date is set.

A review that ends in a table and no action has described the
organization. The findings become decisions of type `review` with a
follow-up, so that the next decision log check reports them until they
are closed, and the next review starts from them.

1. For each failed check, one action per finding: what will be fixed, by
   which role, by when.
2. For each overdue follow-up the log check listed, a new due date or a
   closure.
3. For each question outside the guide, an action naming the document
   and its owner.
4. Set the date of the next review: before the next board meeting, and in
   any case before the re-assessment.

**Worked example.** The review of 2026-01-15 produced four actions. The
legal adviser assigns a licence to the statistical yearbook and the
catalog team adds the structured licence field to it and to the food
security report, due 2026-02-15. The head of methodology reports the
occupation coding agreement by major group at the January board, closing
the follow-up of decision D004. The head of dissemination brings the
first monthly review of the public assistant to the same meeting, closing
the follow-up of decision D007. The Director General's office drafts the
AI strategy document before the re-assessment. The next review is set for
the April board meeting.

**What this does not do.** Actions are promises with dates. The decision
log check of chapter 4 is what keeps them visible, and the board's
schedule is what closes them.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Continuous integration.** The governance files in a repository with the review script as a check on every change turn the review into a gate: a register row without an approval cannot be merged.
- **Scope of the review.** The script reviews this guide's artefacts. An organization with a wider governance function can add its own documents to the list with the questions they answer.
- **Assessment alignment.** The question numbers follow the AI-readiness assessment framework; an organization that uses another framework maps the artefacts to its own questions in the same table.

## Common mistakes

- **A review that reads only the table.** The "present" documents can be stale; the review reads them.
- **Findings without actions.** The review repeats the same finding next year.
- **Evidence assembled on the assessment's eve.** The pack is cheap when the review runs each quarter, and expensive when it is the first look in a year.
- **Questions outside the guide forgotten.** The strategy document and the legal review take longer than any check.

## Verification

- **Review check.** The script runs on the current folder, and every FAIL has an action with a date.
- **Pack check.** Every question of dimensions 1.1, 1.2, and 2.6 has either an artefact with a location and a date or an action with an owner.
- **Cycle check.** The next review date is set and is before the next board meeting.
