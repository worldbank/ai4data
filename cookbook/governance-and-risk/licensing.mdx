# 6. Licences and terms for data and models

**Question:** What do licences and terms of use need to say for AI?

## Rationale

A licence answers the question a user, a researcher, or an AI developer
asks before using a product: what may be done with it. For AI the question
has a new part. A product may be read by an assistant at the time of a
query, which is use, and it may be included in the data a model is trained
on, which is a different use with different consequences. A licence that
is silent on the second leaves the organization's position unknown, and an
AI developer either assumes permission or stays away. The assessment
framework's licensing dimension asks whether the licence is stated, whether
it is attached to each product, whether it is machine-readable, and whether
it covers AI and machine learning explicitly.

The same question runs the other way. The organization uses models that
come with licences, and some of those licences restrict who may use the
model, for what, and how derivatives may be shared. A model whose licence
was not read is a dependence the organization does not understand. This
chapter keeps both tables: the licences the organization gives, and the
licences it has accepted.

## Target state

- Every published product has a licence from a short allowed list, with a URL, stated in the product's record in a machine-readable form.
- Every licence states whether AI use is allowed, allowed under conditions, or prohibited, and the conditions are written.
- Terms of use for machine clients cover identification, attribution, fidelity, and the use of the data for AI.
- Every model the organization uses has a licence class and an allowed-use statement, and the restrictions of restricted licences are noted.

## Maturity levels

| Level | Steps |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Choose a licence for each product type from the allowed list and state the AI-use position for each. |
| **AI-ready** | Put the licence and its URL in every catalog record and API response; publish the terms for machine clients; classify every model licence. |
| **AI-native** | Serve the licence in the structured fields that AI systems read (schema.org, DCAT, Croissant, the MCP server's resources), and run the licence check with every release. |

## Recipes

<Recipe title="6.1 State the licence and the AI-use terms of every product" level="Foundational" skills="Legal adviser with the head of dissemination" time="One hour per product type; minutes per product after that">

**Result:** the organization has one table that gives, for every published
product, its licence, the licence's URL, whether AI use is allowed and
under what terms, and whether the licence is stated in a form machines can
read.

The matrix uses a short allowed list of licences, because a user who meets
one of them knows what it means, and a licence written for one product has
to be read in full by everyone. The list is the open licences the World
Bank and most statistical organizations use, plus "custom" for the access
agreements that microdata need. The AI-use column has three values:
`allowed`, `conditions`, and `prohibited`, and the second and third
require the terms to be written.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `product_id`, `product_type`, `title` | The product and its kind: indicator series, microdata file, geospatial file, publication |
| `licence`, `licence_url` | A licence from the allowed list (CC-BY-4.0, CC0-1.0, CC-BY-SA-4.0, ODC-By-1.0, ODbL-1.0, or custom) and its URL |
| `ai_use`, `ai_use_terms` | Whether AI systems may use the product (allowed, conditions, prohibited) and the terms |
| `machine_readable` | Whether the licence is stated in the product's record in a structured field |

```csv file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/governance-and-risk/licence_matrix.csv
```

1. Decide the licence per product type. Indicator series and
   publications are usually under an attribution licence; public-use
   microdata files are usually under a custom agreement that permits
   analysis and prohibits re-identification and redistribution.
2. Decide the AI-use position per product type. For public indicator
   series, "allowed with attribution" is the position that keeps the
   organization cited. For public-use microdata, "conditions" with the
   same prohibitions as for any user. For licensed files, "prohibited".
3. Put the licence identifier and URL in each product's record in the
   structured field the catalog has: `license` in schema.org, `dct:license`
   in DCAT, `license` in a Croissant record, the licence field in the API
   response.
4. Run the check. It reports a product without a licence, a licence off
   the list, a missing URL, an AI-use position outside the three values,
   conditions without terms, and a licence that is not machine-readable.

```bash
python check_licences.py licence_matrix.csv --models model_licences.csv
```

```text
8 products; licences: 1 (none), 4 CC-BY-4.0, 3 custom
AI use: 4 allowed, 2 conditions, 1 prohibited, 1 unspecified
machine-readable: 6/8
4 models; classes: 2 permissive, 1 proprietary-api, 1 restricted
problem: EX-REP-2025-03: licence is not machine-readable
problem: EX-YB-2023: no licence
problem: EX-YB-2023: AI use 'unspecified' is not allowed, conditions, or prohibited
problem: EX-YB-2023: licence is not machine-readable
result: FAIL
```

**Worked example.** The unemployment rate series and the undernourishment
series are under CC-BY-4.0 with AI use allowed and attribution to the
organization with the series identifier and the release date. The public
use file of the labour force survey's second quarter of 2025 is under a
custom public-use agreement, with AI use allowed under conditions:
training on the file is allowed, re-identification and redistribution are
prohibited. The licensed household budget survey file prohibits AI use,
because its agreement limits use to the approved research purpose. The
check finds two products to fix: the food security report has its licence
on the page and not in the record, and the statistical yearbook has no
licence. The legal adviser assigns CC-BY-4.0 to both, and the catalog
team adds the structured field; the fix is an action of the governance
review in chapter 8.

**What this does not do.** The check verifies that each product has a
reviewed row. Whether a custom licence says what the row claims is the
legal adviser's reading of its text. And a licence states permission; the
[ML-ready datasets](/cookbook/ml-ready-datasets/license) cookbook describes
the disclosure review that decides whether a file may be released for
training at all.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.2 Write terms of use for machine clients" level="AI-ready" skills="Legal adviser with the API or MCP owner" time="Half a day">

**Result:** the organization has published terms that software, including
AI assistants and agents, accepts when it retrieves data from the API or
the MCP server.

The data licence says what may be done with the data. The terms of use say
how the service may be used to get them, and they are where the
organization asks machine clients for the two things that keep official
statistics visible in AI answers: attribution and fidelity. Attribution
means that a value shown to a person carries the series identifier, the
reference period, the release date, and the source. Fidelity means that a
client does not alter a value, a unit, or a period, marks provisional
values as such, and does not substitute a figure from elsewhere under the
organization's name.

1. Start from the running example's
   [terms](pathname:///cookbook-files/governance-and-risk/terms_machine_clients.md)
   and keep the seven sections: scope, registration, attribution,
   fidelity, use of the data for AI, limits and changes, contact.
2. Tie the AI-use section to the licence matrix: training is governed by
   the licence of each product, as the record states.
3. Publish the terms with the API documentation and as a resource of the
   MCP server, so that a client reads them where it connects.
4. State the notice period for changes and link the deprecation policy.

**Worked example.** The example organization's terms, version 1.0 of
2025-12-01, require a client identifier in every request, limit anonymous
access to the public tier, and give registered clients a higher rate limit
and a contact for notices. A client that presents a value shows the series
identifier, the period, the release date, and the source URL that came
with it; a client that generates text attributes it to the organization. A
client that cannot retrieve a value says so. Training is governed by the
licence of each product. Endpoints are withdrawn with at least six months'
notice. The terms are a resource of the MCP server described in the
[Serving Statistics to AI Agents](/cookbook/serving-statistics-to-agents/access)
cookbook.

**What this does not do.** Terms bind clients that accept them; a client
that scrapes the website accepts nothing. The attribution and fidelity
that the terms ask for are also built into the responses themselves
(provenance in every response, verified numbers), so that a well-behaved
client gets them without reading the terms.

</Recipe>

<Recipe title="6.3 Classify the licence of every model the organization uses" level="AI-ready" skills="Data scientist with the legal adviser" time="One hour per model">

**Result:** every model the organization runs or calls has a row with its
licence, a class, an allowed-use statement, and, for restricted licences,
the restrictions written out.

Model licences come in three classes. Permissive licences (Apache-2.0,
MIT) allow internal and commercial use, modification, and redistribution
with notice. Restricted licences, often called community licences, allow
use below a threshold of users or revenue, carry an acceptable-use policy,
and attach their terms to derivatives. Proprietary terms of service govern
hosted models and say what may be sent and what may be done with outputs.
The class decides what the organization may do: adapt the model, share an
adapted model with another statistical organization, or publish outputs.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `model_id`, `model`, `used_by` | The model and the components that use it |
| `licence`, `licence_class` | The licence name and its class: permissive, restricted, or proprietary-api |
| `allowed_use` | What the organization may do under it, in its own words |
| `restrictions_noted` | The restrictions that matter, written out for restricted and proprietary licences |

```csv file=../../website/static/cookbook-files/governance-and-risk/model_licences.csv
```

1. For each model in the register and each candidate under evaluation,
   find the licence text at its source.
2. Classify it, and write the allowed use in the organization's own
   words. For a restricted licence, note the threshold, the acceptable-use
   policy, and the terms for derivatives. For a hosted model, note what
   may be sent and what may be done with outputs.
3. Run the licence check with `--models`. It reports a model without a
   class, without an allowed-use statement, or restricted without its
   restrictions noted.
4. A model whose licence does not allow the intended use is not a
   candidate, whatever its scores.

**Worked example.** The example organization's open-weight 8B model and its
embedding model are under Apache-2.0 and MIT, permissive, which allows the
adaptation and the sharing of adapted models with partner organizations
that chapter 8 of this guide and the roadmap of the program count on. The
hosted model is under proprietary terms that prohibit personal data in
inputs and allow publication of outputs with review, which matches the
component's use on aggregate tables. The 70B candidate is under a custom
community licence, restricted: internal use is allowed below the user
threshold, an acceptable-use policy applies, and a redistributed
derivative carries the licence. The data scientist noted this before the
candidate entered the suite, as the
[Small and Open Models](/cookbook/small-and-open-models/candidates)
cookbook recommends.

**What this does not do.** The class says what the licence allows. Whether
the model's weights are what they claim to be is the file verification of
the small models cookbook, and whether the model is good enough is its
suite.

</Recipe>

## Implementation options

- **Licence identifiers.** The [SPDX licence list](https://spdx.org/licenses/) gives a short identifier for each open licence, which is what the `licence` column and the structured fields carry.
- **Structured fields.** `license` in [schema.org Dataset](https://schema.org/Dataset), `dct:license` in [DCAT](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/), `license` in a [Croissant](https://mlcommons.org/croissant/) record, and the licence field of the API's provenance all take the licence URL.
- **Custom agreements for microdata.** The access tiers of the [microdata documentation](/cookbook/microdata-documentation/access) cookbook (public use, licensed, secure) each carry their own agreement, and the matrix records the AI-use position per tier.
- **Responsible AI licences.** [OpenRAIL](https://www.licenses.ai/) licences attach use restrictions to models and derivatives; they are restricted in the classification above.

## Common mistakes

- **A licence on the website and not in the record.** An AI system reads the record. The page is for people.
- **Silence on AI use.** A licence that says nothing leaves the organization's position unknown and its data either over-used or avoided.
- **One custom licence per dataset.** Every user reads every one. A short allowed list is what makes a licence mean something.
- **Model licences read after the evaluation.** A model that scored well under a licence that forbids the use has cost a week of evaluation for nothing.

## Verification

- **Matrix check.** The licence check passes, or its findings are actions in the decision log with dates.
- **Record check.** For a product picked at random, the licence URL in its catalog record and in its API response equals the matrix.
- **Terms check.** The terms for machine clients resolve from the API documentation and from the MCP server's resources.
- **Model check.** Every model in the register has a row in the model licence table with its class.
