# Practical Guide to Governing AI Use in a Statistical Organization

**A cookbook for management, policy owners, and legal advisers in national statistical organizations.** Version 0.1 (working draft), October 2026.

:::note[Working draft]
This guide is a working draft from the AI for Data – Data for AI program. It
collects practical approaches and open standards. It does not
represent official World Bank Group guidance or policy and is not legal
advice; an organization applies it under its own statistical law, data
protection rules, and institutional policies.
Comments and corrections are welcome as
[GitHub issues](https://github.com/worldbank/ai4data/issues), and the
[contributing](./contributing.md) page explains how to propose a recipe.
:::

The other cookbooks of this series describe what a statistical organization
can do with models: code responses, draft metadata, answer questions, serve
data to agents. Each of them ends with a chapter on the governance its
components need. This guide collects that governance in one place and
widens it to the whole organization. It describes eight instruments that
together let an organization say, for every AI component it runs, what it
is allowed to do, who owns it, how much harm it could do and what controls
that requires, who approved it and why, what the organization depends on
to run it, under what licence its data and its models are used, how its
use is reported to users, and whether all of this is in order before an
AI-readiness assessment.

The instruments are small. The policy is a page; the register, the risk
register, the decision log, the provider register, the cost record, and
the licence matrix are tables with a dozen columns; the statement of model
use is generated from two of them; the review is a script that runs the
checks of the other chapters. What makes them governance is that they are
kept, that they refer to each other, and that a board with written terms
of reference decides from them. A statistical organization that keeps them
can answer an auditor, a user, or an assessor with documents.

The guide follows the Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics and the
AI-readiness assessment framework's dimensions on strategy and governance,
the legal environment, and data licensing. The [standards page](./standards.md)
lists the management, risk, quality, and licensing standards each chapter
builds on.

:::tip[What governance is for]
Every recipe in this guide produces a document or a table that someone
reads before a decision. None of them makes a decision. The board decides
whether a component runs; the Director General accepts the residual risk of
a High-tier component; the legal adviser reads a licence. The instruments
make sure that the decision is taken by the right person with the facts in
front of them, and that it can be found afterwards.
:::

## Prerequisites

The recipes assume the following are in place or can be arranged:

- At least one AI component in use or proposed, and a person in management who will own the AI-use policy.
- A head of methodology or quality who can keep registers and run short scripts, and a legal adviser who can read licences and contract terms.
- For the AI-ready level, evaluation suites for the components in the Medium and High tiers (the [Evaluation Suites](/cookbook/evaluation-suites/) cookbook) and the production and dissemination cookbooks' checks that produce the measures the statement of model use reports.

## First steps

For a team with limited time, this sequence gives the most useful result first:

1. Write the AI-use policy from the template and decide the task boundary, following [chapter 1](./position.mdx). This takes a day and a half.
2. Build the register from an inventory of the components that exist, following [chapter 2](./register.mdx), and rate each one, following [chapter 3](./risk.mdx). This takes an hour per component.
3. Constitute the board and start the decision log with the policy's adoption and the approval of each existing component, following [chapter 4](./decisions.mdx). This takes a day.

The provider register, the licence matrix, and the statement of model use ([chapters 5](./providers.mdx) to [7](./statement.mdx)) follow over the next weeks, and the review of [chapter 8](./review.mdx) runs before the first board meeting of the next quarter.

## Self-assessment

The [self-assessment](./start-here.mdx) takes about five minutes and places
an organization's governance of AI use at a level in each chapter.

## Maturity levels

Each chapter describes three levels. An organization can sit at different
levels in different chapters, and the first level is a complete, useful
state on its own.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foundational** | The instrument exists in a form a person can read: a policy, a table, a log, with an owner and a date. |
| **AI-ready** | The instruments refer to each other and are checked by script before each board meeting and each release, so that a decision starts from their results. |
| **AI-native** | The instruments are generated and checked automatically from the register and the evaluation suites, and their summaries are published with the statement of model use. |

## Running example

The example organization runs six AI components: metadata description
drafts, catalog semantic search, occupation coding, edit failure
explanations, release commentary drafts, and a public statistics
assistant. Five run on its own server; one runs at a hosted provider on
aggregate tables. The guide follows the organization from the adoption of
its policy on 2025-09-15, through the approval of each component, one
incident caught before publication, the statement of model use of the
labour market report of the second quarter of 2025, to its governance
review on 2026-01-15. All components, decisions, dates, and figures are
invented, and the figures of the running example's series are those of
the shared example of this series.

## Scope of the examples

The examples are small on purpose: six components, ten decisions, eight
products, so that a reader can run them in an afternoon and see the shape
of each check. They are starting points. Each recipe ends with a
**What this does not do** note that says where the example stops and what
the full version adds. The scripts check presence, consistency, and
arithmetic; the judgments (the ratings, the approvals, the licence choices)
are the organization's.
