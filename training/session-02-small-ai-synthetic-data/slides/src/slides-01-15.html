Three options

An organization has three ways to get such a task done.

OPTION 1

By hand

Coders read every response and look up its code.

Coding by hand is slow when a survey or a census brings in responses in the thousands.

OPTION 2

A large AI service

A large model at a service company reads each response and returns a code.

Every response is sent to the company that runs the service.

OPTION 3

A small model of its own

A small model on the office's own computer suggests five codes, and a coder chooses.

The model has to be built first, and building it requires many examples with the right answers.

This session is about option 3 and about where the examples come from.

Take the options one at a time and let the room name the limit of each before you reveal it. Option 2 is what people know from chat assistants. Option 3 is the subject of the session, and its limit sets up the next two parts: why a small model, and why the examples have to be written.

Why a small model

A small model runs inside the organization once it has been built.

Once built
  • It works without an internet connection.
  • It runs without a paid service or a .
  • The documents stay on the organization's own computer.
To build it
  • A large model writes the examples.
  • The runs on a GPU.
  • The model does one task only, and it has to be built for that task.

Building it requires many examples with the right answers.

Be even-handed here. The gains come after the model is built, and the costs come before. The call-out is the bridge to the next part: the examples are the hard requirement, and the next slides show why organizations rarely have them.

The original form

The student learns to match the teacher's probability for every possible answer. The probabilities carry more information than the teacher's top answer alone.

In this session the teacher passes its skill through the examples that it writes.

Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean (2015), "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network." The memory of the teacher is that of its 8-bit version.

Name the method. The teacher is the experienced colleague of the previous slide, and the student is the new staff member. The teacher here is about 190 times the size of the student by number of parameters. In the original form of distillation the student copies the teacher's full set of probabilities. In this session the teacher passes its skill through the examples that it writes, and the student is trained on them.

Labelled data from another source

A large model can rewrite labelled job titles as survey answers.

The job title comes from the job board hahu.jobs (dataset published by Tabiya, CC BY 4.0), with the code assigned by the job board.

Synthetic data can also start from labelled data that we already hold. A job board gives thousands of job titles with a code, and a job title reads differently from a survey answer. We ask a large model to say each title the way a respondent would: in a few words, with the place of work, and as the tasks of the job. The code stays with each answer, and a second model checks it.
