Steps 2 to 4 · The training texts

Three sets of texts take the place of a coded survey in training.

After repeated texts are removed, the model trains on 44,058 different texts.

The job titles come from the job board hahu.jobs (dataset published by Tabiya, CC BY 4.0), and the job board assigned their codes.

We had no survey responses with codes for training. So the model learns from three sets of texts. The first holds job titles that a large model wrote from the definitions of the groups. The second is real: job titles from a job board, with the code that the job board assigned. They are worded as advertisements. The third joins the two ideas: a large model rewrites each real job title as the answers of a respondent, and the code stays with each answer. The next two slides show how the third set was written and checked.

Step 4 · The check

A second model removed about a quarter of the answers.

Gemma 4 31B read each answer without the job title and without the code. It chose among the group of the title, the groups next to it in the classification, groups drawn at random, and the reply that none of the listed groups fits.

A wrong code in training becomes a wrong answer in use, so every answer was checked. A second large model read the answer alone and chose a group. We kept the answer when it chose the group of the job title. Go through the job titles on the right. Short answers were removed most often, because a short answer such as "officer" fits several groups. The check also removes answers that are correct and too short to identify one group among 436.

Step 5 · The training run

In one training run the small model goes from 0 to 68 percent of first codes right.

The scores are the mean of three training runs on 1,000 survey-style test answers. One pass reads 35,515 of the 44,058 training texts, and the other texts are held out.

This is the training run of our small model, recorded at 13 points. Press play. Before training the model gives every answer the same code, and no first code is right. After half of one pass over the training texts it is right for 27 percent of the 1,000 test answers. After one pass it is right for half of them, and after two passes for 63 percent. From there the curve is nearly flat, and the score moves by a few points from one pass to the next. The table shows the same for six of the test answers in one run: a code can be right at one point and wrong at the next. After each pass the model is checked on training texts that it does not learn from. Training stops when that check stops improving, and the best pass is saved. The saved models get 68.4 percent on average. The three runs differ only in their random start.

The original form

The student learns to match the teacher's probability for every possible answer. The probabilities carry more information than the teacher's top answer alone.

In this session the teacher passes its skill through the examples that it writes.

Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean (2015), "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network." The teacher's memory is that of the 8-bit version used for this model.

Name the method. The teacher is about 190 times the size of the student by number of parameters. In the original form of distillation the student copies the teacher's full set of probabilities. In our case the teacher passes its skill through the examples that it writes: the job titles, the answers, and the checks.

Small or large

A small model suits a narrow task that repeats many times.

A small model is a good candidate when
  • the answer comes from a fixed list, such as topics or codes;
  • the same task is repeated many times;
  • the data should stay inside the organization;
  • the internet connection is poor.
A large model is the better choice when
  • the answer is new text, such as a summary or a reply;
  • the questions cannot be listed in advance.

On survey-style answers about occupation, our small model scored about the same as the best of three large models and answered in 0.05 seconds on an ordinary processor. On report topics, a second small model also scored about the same as three large models.

These criteria summarize everything so far. Go back to the survey response from the start: a fixed list of codes, a task that repeats, confidential records. Then reveal the other side. A large model remains the right tool for open questions and for writing. Then reveal the two results. The description of this session says that a specialized model can be more accurate, more efficient, and cheaper than a general-purpose model. Our two tests support the efficiency. On accuracy, both small models scored about the same as the large models, and neither scored clearly higher. The occupation test has a weakness, because a large model wrote its answers. A test on real cases settles the question for each task.

Before relying on a small model

A team checks four points before it relies on a small model.

  1. 1The model was tested on real documents from the place of use, with known answers.
  2. 2The test covers the topics or codes that matter.
  3. 3The score was compared with a large model on the same test.
  4. 4A person reviews a sample of the output at regular intervals.
