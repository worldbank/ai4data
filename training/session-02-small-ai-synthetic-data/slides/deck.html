Building specialized small AI using synthetic data

An introduction to small AI, synthetic data, and model distillation

Tasks for a small model

The same kind of task appears across development work.

The tasks illustrate where the method could apply. They do not describe systems in use. Each practice case was written for this session.

Open two or three of the six tasks, starting with the one closest to the room. For each, read the practice case aloud and point at the answer: it always comes from a list or has a fixed form. Ask the room for a seventh task from their own work and keep it for the quiz near the end.

Three options

An organization has three ways to get such a task done.

OPTION 1

By hand

Staff read every message and sort it.

Sorting by hand is slow when the messages arrive in thousands.

OPTION 2

A large AI service

A large model at a service company reads each message and answers.

Every message is sent to the company that runs the service.

OPTION 3

A small model of its own

A small model runs on the organization's own computer.

The model has to be built first, and building it requires many examples with the right answers.

This session is about option 3 and about where the examples come from.

Take the options one at a time and let the room name the limit of each before you reveal it. Option 2 is what people know from chat assistants. Option 3 is the subject of the session, and its limit sets up the next two parts: why a small model, and why the examples have to be written.

Plan of the session

The session answers five questions in order.

  1. 1Why a small model?

Give the plan in one breath and move on. The strip at the top of each slide shows the current part, so the room always knows where we are. If time is short, parts 1, 2 and 4 carry the core of the session.

Why a small model

A small model runs inside the organization once it has been built.

Once built
  • It works without an internet connection.
  • It runs without a paid service or a .
  • The documents stay on the organization's own computer.
To build it
  • A large model writes the examples.
  • The runs on a GPU.
  • The model does one task only, and it has to be built for that task.

Building it requires many examples with the right answers.

Be even-handed here. The gains come after the model is built, and the costs come before. The call-out is the bridge to the next part: the examples are the hard requirement, and the next slides show why organizations rarely have them.

The missing examples

For these tasks the labelled examples are often missing.

Never collected

Messages sent after a flood do not exist before the flood, and the model has to work on the first day.

Private

Applications and complaints hold personal data. Sending them to an outside service for labelling is often not allowed.

Rare

The cases that matter most are often the rarest, such as an emergency among routine messages.

pls help, maji hayajatoka tangu jumatatu. contractor broke the pipe…

Local language

Real messages mix languages, abbreviations, and spelling errors. Few labelled examples exist in most local languages.

Reveal the four reasons one at a time and ask which one applies to the room's own work. Usually more than one does. The flood example is the clearest: the data do not exist until the day the model is needed. The next slide looks closer at the third reason.

The process

The process from a task to a small model has nine steps.

Write the cases
Train and test
Improve and maintain

We follow one example through the nine steps: complaints sent by text message to a project.

Here is the whole process before we take the steps one at a time. The first four steps produce the cases, the next two build and test the model, and the last three keep improving it. Each number opens its step. We use the complaints example for all nine steps because it is easy to picture.

Step 1 of 9 · Describe the task

The team writes down the possible answers and defines each one.

In an office

The manager writes down the decisions the new staff member will make.

With the models

The team writes the list of possible answers, with a definition of each.

Used in the worked example. A list of 86 topics.

Start with the list of answers. Select two or three issues and read the definitions aloud. Ask the room who would write this list in their organization. The same definitions go to the large model, to the people who check the cases, and to the test.

Step 2 of 9 · Show a few real cases

A few real cases show the large model how real messages are written.

In an office

The colleague who writes the practice cases first reads a few real files.

With the models

A few dozen real examples go into the request to the large model. Personal details are removed first.

Not used in the worked example. The requests named the topics only.

Switch between the two requests. Without samples the large model writes polite letters, and nobody sends a letter by text message. With three real messages in the request, the written cases take on their style. The worked example skipped this step.

Step 3 of 9 · Vary the cases

Each request to the large model asks for a different kind of case.

In an office

The colleague writes cases of every kind: short and long, calm and angry, easy and hard.

With the models

Each request sets a different combination of answer, length, style, language, and difficulty.

Partly used in the worked example. Each request named a different set of eight topics and asked for varied length and style.

Turn on the attributes one at a time and watch the number of possible requests grow to 864. Draw a few requests. Then point at the measured result below: asked for the same thing many times, a large model repeated itself in more than a third of the cases.

Step 4 of 9 · Write and check

A second model checks every case before it is used.

In an office

A second colleague reads each practice case and its answer before it is used.

With the models

A second model labels each case on its own. Scripts remove repeats, answers outside the list, and cases where the two models disagree.

Used in the worked example. Scripts checked every set. In the later rounds, a second model also labelled each sentence.

Run the checks and count the cases that remain. Each removed case has its reason next to it. The third row matters most: the writer was wrong about its own case, and the second model caught it. A wrong answer in training becomes a wrong answer in use.

Step 5 of 9 · Train the small model

The small model studies the checked cases until its answers match.

In an office

The new staff member studies the practice cases.

With the models

The small model is trained on the checked cases until its answers match theirs.

Used in the worked example. About 20,000 sentences in the first set.

This step is short on the slide and long in practice. The checked cases go in, and a model comes out. We will train a small model live on a later slide, so I will take questions about learning there.

Step 6 of 9 · Test on real cases

The test uses real cases that no model has trained on.

In an office

Before the new staff member works alone, a supervisor checks their decisions on real past files.

With the models

A few hundred real cases, with answers given by people, are set aside for testing only.

Used in the worked example. 300 real report abstracts with recorded topics.

Turn the switch on and ask the room what is wrong with the picture. A test on practice cases measures how well the small model copies the writing habits of the large model. The real cases are set aside before anything is written, and no model trains on them.

Step 7 of 9 · Practise the weak spots

Extra cases help most on the kinds of cases the model gets wrong.

In an office

The colleague writes more practice cases of the kinds the new staff member gets wrong.

With the models

A program finds where the model makes most mistakes and asks the large model for more cases of those kinds. The model is trained again.

Used in the worked example. Up to five rounds, compared with rounds of the same size spread evenly over all topics.

Read the loop first. Then compare the three bars: the first set, extra cases spread evenly, and extra cases aimed at the mistakes. The targeted rounds reach the orange band, which is the level of the large models. The second repeat of the experiment gives the same picture.

Step 8 of 9 · Ask people which is better

Experienced staff choose the better of two cases.

In an office

Experienced staff compare two practice cases, or two answers, and say which one is better.

With the models

People choose between pairs. The choices improve the request to the large model and filter the cases.

Not used in the worked example. Not part of the experiments.

Run this as a vote. Read each pair and ask the room for A or B before you select one. People who know the work can tell within seconds which case is realistic, and choosing between two is quicker than writing a case.

Step 9 of 9 · Watch and refresh

A model in use needs regular review by people.

In an office

The supervisor keeps reviewing a sample of the staff member's decisions.

With the models

People review a sample of the model's answers at regular intervals. When the answers, the language, or the situation change, new cases are written and the model is trained again.

Not used in the worked example. Outside the experiments.

The example from the report is about images. The same thing happens with text when a new issue or a new language appears. Ask the room who would review the sample in their organization after the project ends.

In distillation a large model passes a skill to a small one.

The original form

The student learns to match the teacher's probability for every possible answer. The probabilities carry more information than the teacher's top answer alone.

In this session the teacher also writes the examples.

Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean (2015), "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network." The teacher's memory is that of the 8-bit version used in the example.

Name the method. The teacher is about 190 times the size of the student by number of parameters. In the original form of distillation the student copies the teacher's full set of probabilities. In our case the teacher has a second job, because it writes the training sentences as well.

Speed and computer

The student answers in 0.19 seconds on an ordinary processor.

The times of the hosted services include the network and the service's queue. The CPU time was measured with four threads of a server processor on 100 of the abstracts, and the time of Qwen3.8-27B on 50.

The axis is logarithmic, so each step to the right is ten times slower. The student on an ordinary processor is about 3 to 20 times faster than the large models, and those ran on a GPU or through an online service. This is the practical gain: the model fits on a laptop and needs no connection.

Small or large

A small model suits a narrow task that repeats many times.

A small model is a good candidate when
  • the answer comes from a fixed list, such as topics or codes;
  • the same task is repeated many times;
  • the data should stay inside the organization;
  • the internet connection is poor.
A large model is the better choice when
  • the answer is new text, such as a summary or a reply;
  • the questions cannot be listed in advance.
These criteria summarize everything so far. Go back to the complaint from the start: a short list of answers, a task that repeats, private messages. Then reveal the other side. A large model remains the right tool for open questions and for writing.

Before relying on a small model

A team checks four points before it relies on a small model.

  1. 1The model was tested on real documents from the place of use, with known answers.
  2. 2The test covers the topics or codes that matter.
  3. 3The score was compared with a large model on the same test.
  4. 4A person reviews a sample of the output at regular intervals.
