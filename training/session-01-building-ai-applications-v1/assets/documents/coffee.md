# Coffee exports in East Africa

Coffee is one of the traditional export crops of Kenya and Rwanda. Both countries grow mainly arabica varieties at altitude, and both have moved part of their production towards specialty grades that sell at a premium in export markets.

In Kenya, coffee is grown by cooperatives of smallholders and by estates, and most of the crop is sold through a central auction. In Rwanda, washing stations established since the early 2000s have raised the share of fully washed coffee, and the sector is organized around cooperatives and private exporters.

Export earnings in both countries depend on world prices, on the volume of the harvest, and on the share of the crop that reaches specialty grades. Weather, plant disease, and the age of the trees affect volumes from year to year.

This note describes the sector in general terms and carries no figures.
