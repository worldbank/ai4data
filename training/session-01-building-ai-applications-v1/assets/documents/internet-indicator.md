# How the internet-users indicator is defined and measured

## Definition

The indicator "Individuals using the Internet (% of population)" counts individuals who have used the internet from any location in the last three months, as a share of the total population. Use through any device counts: a computer, a mobile phone, a games console, a digital television, or another device.

## Source

The values come from the World Telecommunication/ICT Indicators Database of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU). National figures are collected by the ITU from national statistical offices and regulators, through household surveys where they exist and through estimates where they do not.

## What to keep in mind when comparing countries and years

- Survey-based values and estimated values can sit side by side in the same series. The ITU marks the method in its own database; the World Development Indicators carry the value without that mark.
- The reference period is three months, so occasional use counts. The indicator says nothing about frequency, speed, or cost of access.
- Changes in survey design can produce a break in a series. A jump between two years can be a measurement change as much as a change in use.
- The indicator is a share of the whole population, including children. Shares among adults are higher.

## Related indicators

Mobile cellular subscriptions per 100 people measure subscriptions, which can exceed the number of people. Fixed broadband subscriptions per 100 people measure connections, which are shared within households. None of the three measures the same thing as the share of individuals using the internet.
