# Mobile money in Kenya and Rwanda

Mobile money services let a subscriber hold a balance on a mobile phone account, send money to other subscribers, pay merchants and bills, and withdraw cash through agents. Kenya's first large service launched in 2007 and became the reference case for the model. Rwanda's services grew later and faster, supported by interoperability between operators and by the use of mobile payments for public fees and transport.

Survey evidence in both countries indicates that most adults use a mobile money account, and that for many it is the only financial account they hold. Agent networks extend into rural areas where bank branches are absent.

Mobile money runs on basic phones through USSD menus as well as on smartphones through applications. Use of the service is therefore possible without internet access, and the share of adults using mobile money differs from the share of individuals using the internet. The two indicators answer different questions.

This note contains no figures. Indicator values for mobile money accounts are published in the Global Findex database and in national financial-inclusion surveys.
